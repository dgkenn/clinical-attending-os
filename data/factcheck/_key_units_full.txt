=== curated-phase2_unit_198 | RRT Indications - When to Start and Relative Contraindicatio | score=0.768
RRT indications (RIFLE/KDIGO based): (1) Refractory hyperkalemia (K+ >6.5 refractory to medical management). (2) Refractory pulmonary edema/fluid overload (oliguria, high fluid requirements). (3) Severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1 refractory). (4) Uremia symptoms (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding). (5) Severe electrolyte abnormalities (hyponatremia, hypocalcemia unresponsive to treatment). Relative contraindications: hemodynamic instability (relative; CRRT preferred over intermittent), active bleeding (if anticoagulation for CRRT needed; heparin-free circuits possible), thrombocytopenia <50k (bleeding risk with anticoagulation). Early RRT (within 24-48h of AKI diagnosis) may improve outcomes in severe AKI (KDIGO 3) based on recent trials; avoid delay if indications present.

=== curated-phase2_unit_199 | Hyperkalemia - EKG Changes and Treatment Algorithm | score=0.870
Hyperkalemia: K+ >5.5 mEq/L. Symptoms: weakness, palpitations, syncope (rare until K+ >7). EKG changes (progressive): peaked T waves (K+ 5.5-6.5), prolonged PR/widened QRS (K+ 6.5-7.5), loss of P wave/bradycardia, sine wave (K+ >8, cardiac arrest imminent). Absent symptoms/EKG doesn't exclude danger (can progress rapidly). Management algorithm: (1) Calcium gluconate 10-20 mL of 10% IV bolus (cardiac membrane stabilization, no K+ lowering, 30-60 min duration). (2) Insulin 10 units IV + dextrose 25g (50 mL D50W) = shift K+ intracellularly, effect 10-20 min, lasts 4-6 hours. (3) Beta-2 agonist (albuterol 10-20 mg nebulized) = K+ shift, slower onset. (4) Sodium bicarbonate 1-2 amps IV (raises pH, K+ shift), use if acidemia. (5) Diuretics (furosemide) if euvolemic. (6) RRT if refractory/oliguric. (7) Cation exchange resin (kayexalate, sodium polystyrene sulfonate) = slow K+ removal via GI excretion.

=== curated-phase2_unit_201 | Symptomatic vs Asymptomatic Hyponatremia - Acute vs Chronic | score=0.720
Symptomatic hyponatremia: nausea, headache, confusion, lethargy, seizures (Na+ <120), coma, death (if severe <110). Acute hyponatremia (<48h): cells haven't adapted, increased intracellular fluid volume -> cerebral edema risk at lower Na+ levels. Chronic hyponatremia (>48h): osmolytes extruded from cells, cerebral edema less likely even if Na+ very low. Correction rate critical: too-rapid correction (>12 mEq/L in 24h) risks central pontine myelinolysis (demyelination, locked-in syndrome, death). Acute symptomatic: 3% hypertonic saline (NaCl 3%, 1 mL/kg IV bolus, repeat once if still seizing), aim for 4-6 mEq/L rise per hour. Chronic asymptomatic: fluid restriction (SIADH), slow correction 4-8 mEq/L per 24h.

=== curated-phase2_unit_202 | Hyponatremia Treatment - 3% Saline, Fluid Restriction, Vapta | score=0.795
3% hypertonic saline (NaCl 3%): reserved for symptomatic acute hyponatremia. Dosing: 1-2 mL/kg IV bolus (50-100 mL), repeat once after seizure cessation, then reassess. Calculate Na+ rise: change in Na+ = ([Na+ infused] - [Na+ serum]) / (total body water + 1). Example: 100 mL 3% saline (513 mEq/L) in 70 kg patient (TBW ~42L) = ~1 mEq/L rise. Fluid restriction: first-line for SIADH, restrict to 500-800 mL/day. Vaptans (tolvaptan, conivaptan): vasopressin antagonists, aquaretics (promote free water excretion). Used in SIADH refractory to fluid restriction; oral or IV. Caution: rapid Na+ correction risk, expensive, hepatotoxicity monitoring needed. Monitor: serum Na+ q2-4h during acute treatment, avoid hypernatremia (over-correction), neuro checks.

=== curated-phase2_unit_203 | Hypernatremia - Causes and Free Water Deficit Calculation | score=0.731
Hypernatremia: Na+ >145 mEq/L. Causes: insensible losses (diabetes insipidus, sweating, diarrhea, inadequate intake, hypertonic fluid administration. Diabetes insipidus (DI): central (pituitary damage/surgery, hypothyroidism, trauma, idiopathic) vs nephrogenic (congenital, lithium, hypercalcemia, CKD). Presentation: polyuria, polydipsia (if intact thirst), hypernatremia (if intake insufficient), hypertonicity symptoms (restlessness, confusion, lethargy). === curated-phase2_unit_204 | Correction Rate Calculations - Avoiding Osmotic Demyelinatio | score=0.795
Osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS): central pontine + extrapontine myelinolysis from overly rapid Na+ correction (>12 mEq/L in 24h). Risk: chronic hyponatremia (cells adapted), rapid correction re-creates osmotic gradient = cellular dehydration -> demyelination. Symptoms: flaccid paralysis, locked-in appearance, death (acute), behavioral changes/movement disorders (delayed recovery phase). Prevention: limit correction to 4-8 mEq/L per 24h (chronic) or 10-12 mEq/L per 24h (acute symptomatic). Monitor: serum Na+ q4-6h during acute correction, adjust infusion rate. If Na+ rising too fast: stop hypertonic saline, reduce rate, use dextrose (free water) to slow correction. Treat underlying disorder (SIADH = fluid restriction, DI = vasopressin). ODS irreversible; prevention critical.

=== curated-phase3_unit_13 | Crystalloid Fluid Resuscitation - Initial Bolus | score=0.719
First-line fluid for septic shock: crystalloid IV bolus 30 mL/kg body weight over 1-2 hours (e.g., 2.1 L for 70 kg patient) if hypotensive or lactate â‰¥4 mmol/L. Monitor for improvement: SBP >90, MAP >65, HR slowing, improved perfusion (warm extremities, capillary refill <2s). Fluid responsiveness assessed by: response to 500 mL bolus (SBP rise >10 mmHg or HR drop >10 bpm); pulse pressure variation <13% on ventilator; inferior vena cava collapsibility >50%. If no improvement after 30 mL/kg, escalate vasopressors. Reassess for over-resuscitation (pulmonary edema, increasing lactate from gut ischemia).

=== curated-phase3_unit_14 | Vasopressor Selection & Dosing | score=0.795
After fluid resuscitation, if MAP <65 mmHg despite fluids, initiate vasopressor: (1) Norepinephrine (Levophed) = first-line ONLY agent (alpha + beta effects, improves mortality vs. dopamine). Dose 0.01-0.5 mcg/kg/min; target MAP â‰¥65. (2) Epinephrine = added if norepinephrine + low cardiac output. (3) Dopamine = NO LONGER RECOMMENDED as first-line (increased arrhythmia risk vs. NE). (4) Phenylephrine = pure alpha-agonist, use ONLY if inadequate perfusion contraindication (e.g., ACS). (5) Vasopressin = adjunct to catecholamines in refractory shock, no mortality benefit. Tie to arterial line for continuous monitoring; reassess after each dose change. Wean as MAP/perfusion improves.

=== curated-phase3_unit_16 | Corticosteroids in Sepsis & Septic Shock | score=0.782
Role of corticosteroids in sepsis remains controversial. Evidence: low-dose hydrocortisone (50 mg q6h) may benefit refractory septic shock (persistent hypotension despite fluids + vasopressors, especially with adrenal insufficiency). CORTICUS trial showed modest benefit in vasopressor-dependent shock. Mechanism: restore vascular tone, improve myocardial contractility. Typical approach: if vasopressor requirement escalating or prolonged (>48h), consider hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h for 7 days OR methylprednisolone 1-2 mg/kg for 3 days. Avoid steroids in early sepsis without refractory shock (may worsen outcomes in milder cases). Screen for adrenal insufficiency (ACTH stimulation) if prolonged ICU stay.

=== curated-phase3_unit_88 | Eclampsia & Pre-eclampsia - Hypertension in Pregnancy | score=0.647

=== curated-phase3_unit_89 | IV Antihypertensive Agents - Pharmacology & Dosing | score=0.683
(1) Labetalol: combined alpha/beta blockade. IV bolus 10-20 mg, repeat q10-20 min (max 300 mg), OR infusion 1-2 mg/min titrated. Onset 5-10 min, duration 2-4h. (2) Nicardipine: calcium channel blocker. Infusion 5-15 mg/h, titrate q5-15 min. Onset 15-30 min, duration 30 min post-infusion. (3) Hydralazine: direct arterial vasodilator. IV bolus 5-20 mg q30 min, onset 10-20 min. (4) Esmolol: short-acting beta blocker. IV bolus 80-500 mcg/kg, then infusion 50-300 mcg/kg/min. (5) Nitroprusside: potent balanced vasodilator. IV infusion 0.3-10 mcg/kg/min, AVOID >4 mcg/kg/min >4h (cyanide toxicity). (6) Immediate-release nifedipine: oral sublingual 10-20 mg, unpredictable absorption, outdated.

=== curated-phase3_unit_127 | Osmotic Therapy for Cerebral Edema | score=0.652
Mannitol 20%: osmotic diuretic, draws fluid from brain interstitium into vasculature, lowers ICP. Dose: 0.5-1 g/kg IV over 15-30 min, repeat q6-8h. Onset 20-30 min, duration 4-6h. Monitoring: osmolality (limit <320 mOsm/kg risk organ failure), renal function, serum Na. Complication: rebound increase in ICP if mannitol crosses disrupted BBB. Hypertonic saline (3%): sodium draws fluid osmotically. Dose: 30 mL bolus or 0.1-1 mL/kg/h infusion. Advantage: no renal side effects, may improve immune function. Both equally effective; choice often institutional. Avoid both agents if severe hyperchloremic acidosis. Limit osmolality <320.

=== curated-phase3_unit_131 | First-Line Treatment - Benzodiazepines | score=0.759
Goal: stop seizures within minutes. IV benzodiazepines most effective first-line: (1) Lorazepam (Ativan): 4 mg IV bolus, repeat q2-5 min (max 8 mg); onset <1 min, duration 30-60 min. (2) Midazolam IM: 10 mg IM (if no IV access), onset 5-10 min. (3) Diazepam (Valium): 5-10 mg IV q5-10 min (max 30 mg); older, longer-acting, less preferred. Lorazepam preferred (balanced onset/offset). Efficacy: 60-80% seizures stop within 5 min of lorazepam. Monitor: respiratory depression (may need intubation), hypotension. Have equipment/drugs ready for airway management.

=== curated-phase3_unit_132 | Second-Line Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs) | score=0.733
If seizures persist >5 min after benzodiazepine: (1) Fosphenytoin (Cerebyx): 15-20 PE/kg IV (phenytoin equivalent) at 100-150 mg/min; onset 30-60 min, long half-life (24h). Loading: 20 PE/kg, then maintenance 5 mg/kg/day divided. Phenytoin (Dilantin): older formulation, IV irritating, slower replacement kinetics. (2) Levetiracetam (Keppra): 500-2000 mg IV q12h; onset 15-30 min, fewer drug interactions, well-tolerated. (3) Valproic acid: 20-40 mg/kg IV; onset 15-30 min, effective but risk hepatotoxicity (avoid in liver disease). (4) Lacosamide: 200 mg IV; limited experience. Any agent acceptable; no clear superiority. Combination (e.g., lorazepam + fosphenytoin + levetiracetam) sometimes used.
