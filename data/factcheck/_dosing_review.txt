=== curated-phase2_unit_199 | Hyperkalemia - EKG Changes and Treatment Algorithm | score=0.870
Hyperkalemia: K+ >5.5 mEq/L. Symptoms: weakness, palpitations, syncope (rare until K+ >7). EKG changes (progressive): peaked T waves (K+ 5.5-6.5), prolonged PR/widened QRS (K+ 6.5-7.5), loss of P wave/bradycardia, sine wave (K+ >8, cardiac arrest imminent). Absent symptoms/EKG doesn't exclude danger (can progress rapidly). Management algorithm: (1) Calcium gluconate 10-20 mL of 10% IV bolus (cardiac membrane stabilization, no K+ lowering, 30-60 min duration). (2) Insulin 10 units IV + dextrose 25g (50 mL D50W) = shift K+ intracellularly, effect 10-20 min, lasts 4-6 hours. (3) Beta-2 agonist (albuterol 10-20 mg nebulized) = K+ shift, slower onset. (4) Sodium bicarbonate 1-2 amps IV (raises pH, K+ shift), use if acidemia. (5) Diuretics (furosemide) if euvolemic. (6) RRT if refractory/oliguric. (7) Cation exchange resin (kayexalate, sodium polystyrene sulfonate) = slow K+ removal via GI excretion.

=== curated-phase2_unit_202 | Hyponatremia Treatment - 3% Saline, Fluid Restriction, Vapta | score=0.795
3% hypertonic saline (NaCl 3%): reserved for symptomatic acute hyponatremia. Dosing: 1-2 mL/kg IV bolus (50-100 mL), repeat once after seizure cessation, then reassess. Calculate Na+ rise: change in Na+ = ([Na+ infused] - [Na+ serum]) / (total body water + 1). Example: 100 mL 3% saline (513 mEq/L) in 70 kg patient (TBW ~42L) = ~1 mEq/L rise. Fluid restriction: first-line for SIADH, restrict to 500-800 mL/day. Vaptans (tolvaptan, conivaptan): vasopressin antagonists, aquaretics (promote free water excretion). Used in SIADH refractory to fluid restriction; oral or IV. Caution: rapid Na+ correction risk, expensive, hepatotoxicity monitoring needed. Monitor: serum Na+ q2-4h during acute treatment, avoid hypernatremia (over-correction), neuro checks.

=== curated-phase2_unit_204 | Correction Rate Calculations - Avoiding Osmotic Demyelinatio | score=0.795
Osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS): central pontine + extrapontine myelinolysis from overly rapid Na+ correction (>12 mEq/L in 24h). Risk: chronic hyponatremia (cells adapted), rapid correction re-creates osmotic gradient = cellular dehydration -> demyelination. Symptoms: flaccid paralysis, locked-in appearance, death (acute), behavioral changes/movement disorders (delayed recovery phase). Prevention: limit correction to 4-8 mEq/L per 24h (chronic) or 10-12 mEq/L per 24h (acute symptomatic). Monitor: serum Na+ q4-6h during acute correction, adjust infusion rate. If Na+ rising too fast: stop hypertonic saline, reduce rate, use dextrose (free water) to slow correction. Treat underlying disorder (SIADH = fluid restriction, DI = vasopressin). ODS irreversible; prevention critical.

=== curated-phase2_unit_208 | Hypothyroidism Treatment - Levothyroxine Dosing and Monitori | score=0.782
Levothyroxine (synthetic T4): first-line treatment for hypothyroidism. Dosing: 1.6 mcg/kg daily (usually 25-200 mcg depending on baseline TSH/body weight). Start low (25-50 mcg), increase by 25-50 mcg every 4-6 weeks until euthyroid. Take on empty stomach (fasting absorption best, 30-60 min before food/supplements). Recheck TSH 6-8 weeks after dose change (steady state takes 4-6 weeks due to long half-life). Titrate to TSH 0.5-2.5 mIU/L (target patient-specific: lower in post-thyroidectomy/thyroid cancer, elderly prefer TSH 1-3). Monitor annually once stable.

=== curated-phase2_unit_211 | Thyroid Storm - Life-Threatening Emergency | score=0.685
Thyroid storm: decompensated hyperthyroidism, mortality 1-5% even treated, >25% if untreated. Triggers: inadequate antithyroid treatment, infection, surgery/anesthesia in untreated hyperthyroid. Presentation: fever (often high, >38.5C), tachycardia (often >140), atrial fibrillation, altered mental status, thyroid enlargement, GI symptoms (diarrhea, nausea), jaundice, hepatomegaly. Pathophysiology: excess thyroid hormone causing sympathomimetic toxicity, altered TH metabolism. Management: urgent hospitalization, beta-blockers (propranolol 40-80 mg q6h, reduces symptoms + T4->T3 conversion), PTU 150-200 mg q6h (preferred over methimazole in crisis for toxicity profile), Lugol's solution or SSKI 1-2 drops q6-8h (blocks T4 release, start after PTU 1 hour to prevent thyroid hormone synthesis worsening).

=== curated-phase2_unit_212 | PTU vs Methimazole - Dosing and Toxicity Profile | score=0.706
PTU (propylthiouracil): blocks thyroid peroxidase (TPO), reduces T4/T3 synthesis + inhibits T4->T3 conversion (faster symptom improvement). Dosing: 50-100 mg TID (start), taper to 25-50 mg TID maintenance. Risk: hepatotoxicity (0.1-0.3%, can be fulminant), agranulocytosis (rare), rash. Preferred in first trimester pregnancy (crosses placenta less), thyroid storm (faster T4->T3 inhibition). Methimazole: blocks TPO, T4/T3 synthesis only. Dosing: 5-20 mg daily. Risk: agranulocytosis (0.3%), rash, arthralgia. Preferred first-line (once-daily dosing, lower hepatotoxicity). Pregnancy: PTU first trimester, switch methimazole second/third trimester (PTU hepatotoxicity risk, methimazole embryopathy rare). Monitor: CBC baseline, LFTs baseline, repeat if fever/infection/jaundice.

=== curated-phase3_unit_14 | Vasopressor Selection & Dosing | score=0.795
After fluid resuscitation, if MAP <65 mmHg despite fluids, initiate vasopressor: (1) Norepinephrine (Levophed) = first-line ONLY agent (alpha + beta effects, improves mortality vs. dopamine). === curated-phase3_unit_55 | Corticosteroids in ARDS | score=0.819
Controversial in ARDS; limited evidence of benefit. Early corticosteroids (within first 2 weeks): some studies suggest modest PaO2 improvement + shorter ventilation duration, but NO mortality benefit. Risk: increased infection, hyperglycemia. Mechanism: anti-inflammatory, may reduce fibrosis progression. Typical approach: AVOID routine corticosteroids in mild-moderate ARDS. Consider low-dose methylprednisolone 1 mg/kg/day in fibro-proliferative phase (week 2-3) if severe ARDS with persistent organ dysfunction. Avoid very high doses. Monitor glucose, infection risk. === curated-phase3_unit_74 | Diuretic Therapy in Acute Decompensated HF | score=0.769
First-line: IV loop diuretics (furosemide, torsemide, bumetanide). Dosing: start 40-120 mg IV bolus depending on baseline renal function + diuretic dose at home. Goal: negative fluid balance 0.5-1.0 L/day. Monitor: daily weight, I&Os, creatinine, electrolytes (K, Cl, Mg), urine output. === curated-phase3_unit_82 | Cardiogenic Shock - Definition & Management | score=0.770
Cardiogenic shock = low cardiac output state (MAP <65 mmHg OR SBP <90) despite adequate or elevated filling pressures, causing end-organ dysfunction (oliguria, altered MS, elevated lactate). Etiologies: acute MI, acute decompensation HF, myocarditis, stress cardiomyopathy. Presentation: hypotension, cool/clammy extremities, low urine output, altered mental status, elevated lactate. Diagnosis: CVP/PAOP >15 (elevated filling pressure), CI <2.2 L/min/m2 (low cardiac index). Management: (1) IV vasodilators + diuretics cautiously (high risk worsening hypotension); (2) Inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone) + vasopressors (norepinephrine); (3) Mechanical support (IABAP, Impella, ECMO) if refractory. PCI/revascularization if ACS underlying. Mortality 50%+ without mechanical support.

=== curated-phase3_unit_127 | Osmotic Therapy for Cerebral Edema | score=0.652
Mannitol 20%: osmotic diuretic, draws fluid from brain interstitium into vasculature, lowers ICP. Dose: 0.5-1 g/kg IV over 15-30 min, repeat q6-8h. Onset 20-30 min, duration 4-6h. === curated-phase3_unit_131 | First-Line Treatment - Benzodiazepines | score=0.759
Goal: stop seizures within minutes. IV benzodiazepines most effective first-line: (1) Lorazepam (Ativan): 4 mg IV bolus, repeat q2-5 min (max 8 mg); onset <1 min, duration 30-60 min. === curated-phase3_unit_132 | Second-Line Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs) | score=0.733
If seizures persist >5 min after benzodiazepine: (1) Fosphenytoin (Cerebyx): 15-20 PE/kg IV (phenytoin equivalent) at 100-150 mg/min; onset 30-60 min, long half-life (24h). Loading: 20 PE/kg, then maintenance 5 mg/kg/day divided. Phenytoin (Dilantin): older formulation, IV irritating, slower replacement kinetics. (2) Levetiracetam (Keppra): 500-2000 mg IV q12h; onset 15-30 min, fewer drug interactions, well-tolerated. (3) Valproic acid: 20-40 mg/kg IV; onset 15-30 min, effective but risk hepatotoxicity (avoid in liver disease). (4) Lacosamide: 200 mg IV; limited experience. === curated-phase3_unit_133 | Third-Line Treatment - Anesthetic Induction | score=0.610
If seizures refractory after 2 adequate agents: induce anesthesia-level sedation. (1) Propofol: 1-2 mg/kg IV bolus, then 1-10 mg/kg/h infusion; onset <1 min, offset rapid upon cessation. Risk: propofol infusion syndrome (myopathy, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, death) if high dose >4 mg/kg/h x >48h. (2) Midazolam: 0.1-0.2 mg/kg IV bolus, then 0.05-2 mg/kg/h infusion; offset slower. (3) Barbiturates (pentobarbital): loading 5-15 mg/kg, then maintenance; slow offset, risk hypotension. (4) Ketamine: 1-2 mg/kg IV bolus, then 0.5-2 mg/kg/h; preserves airway protective reflexes. Goal: burst suppression on EEG (bursts of activity separated by flat periods = maximal seizure suppression).
