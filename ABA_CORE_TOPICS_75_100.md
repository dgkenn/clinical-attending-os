# ABA Board Exam Core Topics (75-100 Core Domains)
**Comprehensive Anesthesia Attending Knowledge Base**
**Source: ABA Initial Certification Content Outline + ASA Curriculum**

---

## I. PREOPERATIVE EVALUATION & ASSESSMENT (7 topics)

1. **ASA Physical Status Classification System** — PS I-VI categories, emergency modifiers (E), comorbidity severity assessment, day-of-procedure final classification — Know all 6 classes + modifier rules; judge comorbidity threat-to-life severity

2. **History & Physical Examination** — chief complaint, medical history, surgical history, medication review, family history, anesthesia history — Elicit all ASA-relevant comorbidities; screen for difficult airway + previous anesthetic complications

3. **Airway Assessment & Difficult Airway Prediction** — Mallampati classification, thyromental distance, mouth opening, neck mobility, dentition, jaw alignment, upper airway obstruction signs — Predict difficult laryngoscopy vs. difficult intubation vs. difficult bag-mask ventilation; identify both anticipated and unanticipated difficulty

4. **Cardiovascular Assessment** — hypertension, coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, valvular disease, exercise tolerance — Stratify perioperative cardiac risk; identify need for preoperative testing or optimization

5. **Pulmonary Assessment** — COPD, asthma, obstructive sleep apnea, pulmonary hypertension, smoking history, dyspnea — Assess aspiration risk, airway reactivity, and need for modified anesthesia approach

6. **Renal & Hepatic Function Assessment** — chronic kidney disease, liver disease, glucose control — Determine drug clearance impact; guide fluid management and anesthetic agent selection

7. **Neurological Assessment** — stroke history, seizure disorder, neuromuscular disease, psychiatric medications — Identify altered baseline mental status; screen for malignant hyperthermia family history; assess aspiration risk

## II. ANESTHETIC PHARMACOLOGY (18 topics)

### Induction Agents (4 topics)

8. **Propofol** — mechanism (GABA potentiation), dosing (1-2.5 mg/kg IV), onset (30-40 sec), duration (5-10 min context-dependent), pharmacokinetics (highly protein-bound, rapid redistribution) — Calculate weight-based dose; anticipate hypotension & apnea; recognize propofol infusion syndrome risk at high doses/prolonged infusion

9. **Thiopental & Methohexital** — ultrashort-acting barbiturates, mechanism (GABA), dosing (3-5 mg/kg thio, 1-2 mg/kg metho), kinetics (rapid redistribution, long context-dependent time) — Historical context; know redistribution causes emergence; cardiac depression greater than propofol

10. **Etomidate** — non-barbiturate, GABA agonist, dosing (0.1-0.3 mg/kg IV), onset (10-15 sec), kinetics (fast ester metabolism) — Recognize adrenal suppression risk even single dose; hemodynamic stability advantage; use in shock/unstable patients

11. **Ketamine** — dissociative, NMDA antagonist, dosing (1-2 mg/kg IV, 4-5 mg/kg IM), onset (30 sec IV / 3-5 min IM), kinetics (metabolized to norketamine) — Maintain airway reflexes & spontaneous ventilation; sympathomimetic effects; dissociative emergence delirium; pediatric/trauma use

### Volatile Anesthetics (3 topics)

12. **Desflurane** — pungent, MAC 6%, minimum alveolar concentration, kinetics (rapid uptake/elimination via exhalation) — Airway irritation limits inhalational induction; fast emergence advantage; avoid in malignant hyperthermia-susceptible; hepatic/renal metabolism minimal

13. **Sevoflurane** — sweet-smelling, MAC 2%, kinetics (rapid offset), metabolism (20% hepatic) — Preferred for inhalational induction in pediatrics; fluoride ion toxicity risk if prolonged use; emergence delirium common in children

14. **Isoflurane** — MAC 1.15%, pungent, kinetics (slower offset than sevo/desf), metabolism (minimal) — Cardiovascular depression + vasodilation; maintain cerebral autoregulation; hyperkalemia with succinylcholine

### Opioids (4 topics)

15. **Fentanyl** — synthetic opioid, dosing (1-2 mcg/kg bolus, 1-2 mcg/kg/hr infusion), potency 50-100× morphine, kinetics (rapid onset 3-5 min, offset 30 min context-dependent) — Ultra-short acting; prevent hypertensive response to laryngoscopy; hyperrigidity risk at high bolus doses; remifentanil substitute for rapid offset

16. **Morphine** — natural opioid, dosing (0.1-0.2 mg/kg IV), onset 5-10 min, kinetics (hepatic + renal metabolism, renally eliminated active metabolites) — Slow onset limits induction use; histamine release risk (hypotension); accumulation in renal failure

17. **Hydromorphone & Oxymorphone** — semi-synthetic opioids, potency 5-7× morphine, dosing (0.015-0.03 mg/kg), kinetics (hepatic metabolism) — Intermediate potency; shorter duration than morphine; less histamine release

18. **Remifentanil** — ultra-short ester opioid, dosing (0.5-1 mcg/kg bolus, 0.5-1 mcg/kg/min infusion), kinetics (ester plasma hydrolysis, context-independent offset <5 min) — Unique context-independent pharmacokinetics; postoperative hyperalgesia risk; expensive; no active metabolites

### Neuromuscular Blocking Agents (4 topics)

19. **Depolarizing Agent: Succinylcholine** — dual quaternary amine, dosing (1-1.5 mg/kg IV / 4-5 mg/kg IM), onset (fast 30-45 sec), kinetics (rapid hydrolysis by plasma pseudocholinesterase) — Fasciculations cause hyperkalemia + myalgia; contraindicated in malignant hyperthermia; pseudocholinesterase deficiency prolongation; IM use in pediatric difficult IV access

20. **Non-Depolarizing: Rocuronium** — aminosteroid, dosing (0.6 mg/kg intubating, 0.05 mg/kg maint), onset (60-90 sec), duration (30-40 min), kinetics (hepatic/biliary excretion) — Fast onset rival to succinylcholine; vagolytic effects; potency affected by age/body temperature; reversal with sugammadex (4 mg/kg)

21. **Non-Depolarizing: Vecuronium** — aminosteroid, dosing (0.1 mg/kg), onset (90-120 sec), duration (30-40 min), kinetics (hepatic/biliary) — Vagolytic effects; organ-dependent clearance; age/temperature/acidosis prolong effect

22. **Non-Depolarizing: Cisatracurium & Atracurium** — benzylisoquinolinium compounds, dosing (0.1-0.15 mg/kg), onset (90-120 sec), kinetics (Hofmann elimination + ester hydrolysis = organ-independent) — Unique Hofmann elimination independent of kidney/liver; histamine release possible (atracurium > cisatracurium); organ failure patients advantage

### Reversal Agents (2 topics)

23. **Sugammadex** — selective relaxant binding agent, dosing (4 mg/kg for rocuronium, 16 mg/kg for deep block recovery), onset (90-120 sec) — Rapidly reverses aminosteroids; hypercoagulability + thrombotic events reported; encapsulation mechanism; monitor coagulation in high-risk patients

24. **Neostigmine & Glycopyrrolate** — anticholinesterase + antimuscarinic, dosing (neo 0.04-0.07 mg/kg max 5 mg IV + glyco 0.005-0.01 mg/kg), onset (7-11 min) — Reverses non-depolarizing blocks at partial recovery only; bradycardia requires glycopyrrolate co-administration; slower onset than sugammadex

### Local Anesthetics (1 topic)

25. **Local Anesthetic Pharmacology: Amides vs. Esters** — amides (lidocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine) metabolism hepatic; esters (procaine, cocaine) plasma hydrolysis — Toxicity risk (CNS hyperexcitability → seizure, cardiac dysrhythmia → arrest); maximum safe doses with/without epinephrine; treat LAST with 20% lipid emulsion bolus + CPR

## III. AIRWAY MANAGEMENT & INTUBATION (8 topics)

26. **Anatomy of the Airway** — laryngeal structures (epiglottis, vocal cords, arytenoids, cricoid), pharyngeal spaces, laryngeal innervation (vagus CN X), tracheal bifurcation at T4 — Identify anatomical landmarks for intubation + nerve blocks; understand aspiration pathway

27. **Endotracheal Tube Selection & Design** — tube size (ID/OD), cuff design (high vs. low pressure), Murphy eye, radiopaque marker, material (PVC) — Match size to patient age/sex; cuff pressure management (25-30 cm H2O); recognize tube kinking/obstruction

28. **Laryngoscopy Techniques: Direct Visualization** — direct laryngoscopy (Miller/Macintosh blades), blade selection, proper intubation position (sniffing position), visualization grades (Cormack-Lehane I-IV) — Grade I optimal, Grade III difficult, Grade IV cannot see epiglottis; force should not exceed cricoid pressure 10 N

29. **Difficult Airway Algorithm** — anticipate difficult airway preop, awake fiberoptic intubation, video laryngoscopy (McGrath, GlideScope, C-MAC), supraglottic airways (LMA, King Vision), retrograde intubation, cricothyrotomy — Know decision tree for both elective (awake fiberoptic) and emergency (cricothyrotomy) scenarios; call for help early

30. **Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)** — anesthesia induction + immediate intubation, cricoid pressure application (10 N awake → 10 N after loss of consciousness), no bag-mask ventilation, vacuum suction ready — For full stomach/aspiration risk; minimize time to airway control; recognize when to abandon cricoid if oxygenation failing

31. **Supraglottic Airways** — laryngeal mask airways (LMA Classic/ProSeal/Supreme), King LT, i-gel, insertion technique, ventilation seal pressure, removal criteria — Alternatives to intubation for routine surgery; brain detection of proximal obstruction; maintain spontaneous ventilation or mechanical; monitor for displacement

32. **Awake Fiberoptic Intubation** — patient preparation (topical anesthesia), use of sedation (remifentanil, dexmedetomidine), scope advancement through vocal cords, confirm placement (landmarks + capnography) — Gold standard for anticipated difficult airway; requires skilled personnel; post-op sore throat common

33. **Confirmation of Tube Placement** — visual confirmation of tube passing vocal cords, capnography (gold standard), chest X-ray (position verification), auscultation (bilateral breath sounds) — Esophageal intubation = critical error; waveform capnography essential; right mainstem intubation common (measure 21-23 cm at teeth adult)

## IV. INDUCTION, MAINTENANCE & EMERGENCE (6 topics)

34. **Induction Sequence & Drug Selection** — patient-specific choice (hemodynamically unstable → etomidate, trauma/shocked → ketamine, normal → propofol), pre-oxygenation (100% FiO2 × 3 min or 8 vital capacity breaths), induction dose, loss of consciousness sign — Anticipate airway consequences of each agent; verify loss of protective airway reflexes before intubation; have backup airway plan

35. **Maintenance of Anesthesia** — volatile + nitrous oxide + opioid OR TIVA (propofol + remifentanil infusions), MAC targets (0.6-1.0 MAC for unconsciousness), adjuncts (volatile agent reduction with opioid synergy) — Titrate to clinical signs (heart rate, blood pressure, lacrimation, movement); monitor expired agent concentration; adjust for hepatic/renal clearance

36. **Muscle Relaxant Dosing & Monitoring** — initial intubating dose, maintenance boluses vs. infusion, train-of-four (TOF) monitoring to assess recovery, target TOF ratio >0.9 before extubation — Depolarizing vs. non-depolarizing differences; avoid residual blockade complications (hypoventilation, aspiration); reversal agent selection

37. **Emergence Delirium & Agitation** — common with volatile anesthetics (especially sevoflurane pediatric), ketamine dissociation, prevention (smooth emergence, prevent coughing/straining), management (reassurance, low-dose sedatives if needed) — Distinguish delirium from inadequate anesthesia; protect IV lines/monitors during thrashing; residual anesthetic + hypoxia + hypercarbia + pain as triggers

38. **Extubation Criteria** — patient awake + responsive to commands, spontaneous ventilation adequate (TV >5 ml/kg, RR 8-20), protective airway reflexes intact (cough/gag), oxygenation stable on room air or low O2, no muscular blockade (TOF >0.9) — Suction oropharynx before extubation; prepare for re-intubation; smooth emergence reduces coughing/hypertension/tachycardia

39. **Post-operative Airway Considerations** — airway edema risk (prolonged intubation, difficult airway, anaphylaxis), stridor, post-extubation croup (pediatric), upper airway obstruction signs — Assess for swelling + trial extubation in controlled setting; have re-intubation equipment at bedside; neck extension + mandibular thrust for re-oxygenation

## V. MONITORING & ANESTHETIC DEPTH (7 topics)

40. **Standard Monitors (ASA Mandatory)** — pulse oximetry (SpO2), electrocardiography (ECG), non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP), temperature (core: nasopharyngeal/esophageal/bladder preferred), capnography (ETCO2), ventilator parameters — Monitor continuously throughout case; interpret waveforms + trends; alarm settings age-appropriate

41. **Capnography & Ventilation** — normal ETCO2 (35-45 mmHg), interpretation of waveforms (normal vs. plateau vs. absent vs. rebreathing), hypocarbia (hyperventilation, PE, sepsis) vs. hypercarbia (inadequate ventilation, malignant hyperthermia) — Early sign of malignant hyperthermia is rising ETCO2; disconnection alarm if ETCO2 suddenly absent; rebreathing waveform suggests failed CO2 absorption

42. **Neuromuscular Monitoring** — train-of-four (TOF) stimulation, post-tetanic potentiation (PTP) for deep block assessment, clinical assessment inadequate for detecting residual blockade — Apply to all non-depolarizing agents; target TOF >0.9 before extubation; acceleromyography (gold standard) vs. clinical assessment (unreliable at TOF <0.5)

43. **Anesthetic Depth Monitoring (BIS/Entropy)** — EEG-derived indices (0-100 scale), target 40-60 for general anesthesia, awareness prevention, total IV anesthesia titration advantage — Used primarily for TIVA titration; reduces volatile agent use; prevents awareness but not pain (multimodal anesthesia still required); cost-benefit debated

44. **Invasive Hemodynamic Monitoring** — arterial line (beat-to-beat BP, waveform analysis, blood sampling, VAR = stroke volume variation), central venous line (CVP, fluid status, vasopressor infusion), pulmonary artery catheter (CO, SvO2, PCWP in complex cardiac cases) — Central line placement risky (pneumothorax, infection); use only when benefit justifies risk; interpretation requires clinical context

45. **Temperature Monitoring** — core temperature essential during GA, sites (nasopharyngeal most reliable, esophageal, bladder, rectal), hypothermia consequences (impaired coagulation, shivering hypermetabolism, delayed emergence) — Maintain normothermia (36.5-37.5°C) with active warming (forced air blanket, IV fluid warmer); pediatric patients especially at risk for rapid cooling

46. **Metabolic Monitoring** — arterial blood gas (pH, PaCO2, PaO2, HCO3), electrolytes, glucose (especially diabetic patients + stress response), lactate (inadequate perfusion), base excess — Frequent sampling in long cases or hemodynamic instability; hyperglycemia common intraoperatively; correct electrolyte abnormalities affecting cardiac function

## VI. REGIONAL ANESTHESIA (6 topics)

47. **Local Anesthetic Toxicity (LAST)** — CNS toxicity (restlessness → seizure → unconsciousness), cardiac toxicity (arrhythmia → bradycardia → asystole), risk factors (excessive dose, accidental intravascular injection, epinephrine-containing soln in vessels) — Treatment: stop injection immediately, seizure suppression (benzodiazepine/propofol), 20% IV lipid emulsion 1.5 ml/kg bolus + infusions, prolonged CPR (up to 60 min), ECMO if refractory

48. **Neuraxial Anesthesia: Spinal** — injection of local anesthetic into CSF, onset (5-15 min), duration (1-3 hr depending on agent), dermatomal spread, cardiovascular effects (sympathectomy → hypotension), respiratory effects (high thoracic block → breathing difficulty) — Identify L3-L4 or L4-L5 interspace; penumbra risk of subdural hematoma; post-dural puncture headache (PDPH) 1%; prevention with pencil-point needles

49. **Neuraxial Anesthesia: Epidural** — injection into epidural space (above dura), slower onset (15-30 min) vs. spinal, denser sympathetic block, catheter advantage (continuous infusion), segmental anesthesia possible — Requires threading catheter; epidural hematoma risk especially on anticoagulation; total spinal if accidental dural puncture with bolus dose; monitoring for sensory level progression

50. **Peripheral Nerve Blocks: Upper Extremity** — interscalene (C5-C6, shoulder surgery, phrenic nerve paralysis risk), supraclavicular (C5-T1, hand/wrist), infraclavicular (full upper extremity), axillary (distal arm/hand) — Ultrasound guidance gold standard; needle visualization essential; complications (nerve injury, vascular puncture, pneumothorax with supraclavicular); local anesthetic dose calculation critical

51. **Peripheral Nerve Blocks: Lower Extremity** — femoral (quadriceps, knee, hip), sciatic (tibial + peroneal, foot/ankle, knee), saphenous (medial knee/foot), popliteal fossa (foot/ankle), transversus abdominis plane (TAP, abdominal wall) — Single-shot vs. catheter; ultrasound localization; motor vs. sensory blockade duration differences; compartment syndrome risk if tight dressing post-block

52. **Anticoagulation & Neuraxial Anesthesia** — warfarin (INR <1.5 safe for neuraxial), DOAC perioperative management, heparin timing, aspirin continuation safe — Epidural hematoma risk highest in anticoagulated patients; document baseline neuro exam; early spinal cord compression signs (lower extremity paralysis, bowel/bladder dysfunction) require emergent imaging + decompression

## VII. EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT & COMPLICATIONS (10 topics)

53. **Malignant Hyperthermia (MH)** — pharmacogenetic disorder (RYR1/CACNA1S mutations), triggering agents (succinylcholine + volatile anesthetics), early signs (increased ETCO2, tachycardia, muscle rigidity, temperature rise is LATE sign), treatment (stop triggering agents immediately, hyperventilate 100% O2, dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg IV bolus q5min max 10 mg/kg, active cooling, treat hyperkalemia/arrhythmias, rinse IV lines) — Call for help early; prepare for ICU admission; monitor for recrudescence 24-48 hr; MHAUS hotline 1-800-644-9737; genetic counseling for family; awake patient confirmed susceptibility testing (CHCT, genetic testing)

54. **Anaphylaxis During Anesthesia** — IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity (neuromuscular blockers #1 agent, latex #2, antibiotics, blood products), presentation (cardiovascular collapse, bronchospasm, angioedema, rash, hypoxemia), treatment (stop triggering agent, 100% O2, epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM or 50-100 mcg IV titrated, IV fluids 2-4 L, H1/H2 blockers, corticosteroids, airway management if severe) — Biphasic reactions possible (relapse 1-8 hr); tryptase level 15 min-3 hr post-event confirms diagnosis; skin testing/graded challenge for future anesthetics; consider alternative agents in future (e.g., non-depolarizing instead of succinylcholine)

55. **Aspiration of Gastric Contents** — risk factors (full stomach, emergency surgery, pregnancy, obesity, gastroesophageal reflux, emergency intubation), Mendelson syndrome (acid pneumonitis), presentation (hypoxemia, infiltrates on CXR, fever, bronchospasm, pink frothy sputum in severe cases) — Prevention (NPO status, H2 antagonist/proton pump inhibitor, metoclopramide, rapid sequence intubation with cricoid pressure); treatment (suctioning, 100% O2, supportive care, avoid prophylactic antibiotics, consider high-dose corticosteroids if severe)

56. **Airway Fire (OR fire hazard)** — ignition triangle (fuel + heat source + oxygen), high FiO2 + high inspired O2 at surgical site near open flames/electrocautery, especially in head/neck/airway surgery — Prevention (FiO2 <30% if flammable materials in surgical field, careful cautery use, moistened gauze barrier, standby for intubation); immediate management (stop procedure, extinguish fire with water/saline, remove burning materials, check for thermal injury, establish airway, 100% O2, monitor for edema/obstruction)

57. **Difficult Airway Emergencies: Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate** — failed intubation after induction + loss of consciousness, hypoxemia refractory to bag-mask ventilation, decision point: awaken vs. proceed with emergency airway — Supraglottic airway first (LMA), fiberoptic scope if time permits, emergency surgical airway (cricothyrotomy for adults, tracheostomy generally not feasible in emergency); prepare equipment before induction in high-risk cases

58. **Cardiac Arrhythmias Intraoperatively** — tachycardia (hypovolemia, inadequate anesthesia, pain, hyperthermia, malignant hyperthermia), bradycardia (vagal response to traction, oculocardiac reflex, medication effect), ventricular arrhythmias (hypoxemia, hypercarbia, electrolyte abnormality, myocardial ischemia) — Treat underlying cause; atropine 0.5 mg IV for symptomatic bradycardia; consider ACLS protocols for life-threatening rhythms; prepare defibrillator/epinephrine

59. **Intraoperative Myocardial Ischemia** — presentation (ST changes on ECG, wall motion abnormality on TEE, hemodynamic instability), risk factors (CAD, hypertension, tachycardia, hypotension), management (optimize oxygenation/ventilation, reduce heart rate/contractility with beta-blocker/calcium channel blocker, optimize perfusion pressure, treat anemia, nitroglycerin if tolerated) — High-risk patients benefit from perioperative monitoring (ECG, troponin); avoid tachycardia + hypertension combination

60. **Hypovolemic Shock** — presentation (hypotension, tachycardia, cool/clammy skin, oliguria, altered mental status), intraoperative causes (blood loss, fluid misdistribution, inadequate preop volume repletion) — Aggressive fluid resuscitation (crystalloid 1-2 L bolus), blood products if ongoing hemorrhage (activate massive transfusion protocol if needed), vasopressors if refractory, identify + control bleeding source, treat dilutional coagulopathy

61. **Perioperative Stroke & Thromboembolism** — risk factors (perioperative hypercoagulability, atrial fibrillation, recent surgery, immobility), presentation (focal neurologic deficit, altered consciousness), prevention (mechanical prophylaxis, anticoagulation if indicated, maintain blood pressure) — Rarely diagnosed intraoperatively; post-op neuro exam critical; thromboembolic complications increase ICU admission + morbidity

62. **Perioperative Medication Errors** — wrong drug/dose, right patient verification failure, syringe labeling critical, look-alike vials (neuromuscular blockers vs. other agents), distraction/fatigue increases risk — Prevention (standardized labeling, "challenge-response" verbal confirmation, high-alert medication protocols, double-checks before administration); safety culture emphasis

## VIII. SPECIAL POPULATIONS (8 topics)

63. **Pediatric Anesthesia: Neonates & Infants** — immature physiology (higher metabolic rate, lower functional residual capacity, rapid oxygen desaturation in apnea, larger head/tongue), modified drugs/doses (mg/kg based on age), IV access often difficult (IO if needed), hypothermia common — Inhalational induction common in pediatrics; sevoflurane preferred (less airway irritation); calculate fluid requirements carefully (high maintenance rates); avoid excessive PEEP in immature lungs; emergence delirium frequent

64. **Pediatric Anesthesia: Children (2-12 years)** — cooperative children amenable to topical preparation + inhalational induction, emergence behavioral problems (PAED scale), pain management essential (opioids, regional blocks, non-pharmacologic), separation anxiety common — Parental presence for induction reduces anxiety; prevent emergence delirium (avoid volatile washout, smooth emergence); post-operative nausea/vomiting prophylaxis (dexamethasone, ondansetron); caudal block excellent for lower abdominal/lower extremity surgery

65. **Obstetric Anesthesia: Pregnant Patient** — anatomic changes (enlarged breasts, edematous airway, displaced diaphragm), physiologic changes (increased cardiac output, oxygen consumption, decreased FRC, aspirating supine position), teratogenicity concerns (no proven teratogens at normal anesthetic doses), placental transfer (opioids, volatile agents cross placenta) — Difficult airway more common; position patient left lateral decubitus to prevent aortocaval compression; avoid aortocaval compression; fetal monitoring before anesthesia; spinal anesthesia often preferred for cesarean section; oxytocin + ergot prophylaxis post-delivery

66. **Geriatric Anesthesia** — physiologic decline (reduced cardiac reserve, impaired renal function, decreased hepatic metabolism, cognitive impairment), polypharmacy (drug interactions), comorbidities (hypertension, CAD, diabetes), postoperative delirium risk — Reduced anesthetic requirements (lower MAC, lower induction doses); slow titration; monitor for hypotension; regional anesthesia often beneficial; postoperative cognitive dysfunction risk in major surgery; pain management essential

67. **Obese Patient Anesthesia** — increased aspiration risk, difficult airway (enlarged tongue, neck circumference >40 cm), difficult IV access, different pharmacokinetics (some drugs lipophilic → prolonged effect), increased perioperative risk (hypertension, OSA, thromboembolism) — RSI standard; head-up positioning for induction; avoid excessive opioids; regional anesthesia excellent when feasible; aggressive VTE prophylaxis; avoid postoperative opioid accumulation

68. **Difficult Airway Syndromes: Acromegaly** — abnormal growth hormone (large mandible, protruding jaw, tongue enlargement, vocal cord thickening) — Anticipate difficult intubation; awake fiberoptic gold standard; flexible fiberscope may be difficult if soft tissue bulk; consider smaller ET tube; neck extension + flexion assessment critical

69. **Difficult Airway Syndromes: Rheumatoid Arthritis & Ankylosing Spondylitis** — rheumatoid: cervical instability + atlantoaxial subluxation, temporomandibular joint involvement, cricoarytenoid joint involvement (vocal cord fixation), systemic involvement (renal, cardiac, pulmonary); ankylosing: fixed cervical spine, thoracic kyphosis, reduced mouth opening — Awake fiberoptic intubation; gentle neck movement (avoid hyperextension risking cord compression); pre-operative cervical imaging if available; monitor for cervical myelopathy signs post-operatively

70. **Difficult Airway Syndromes: Pierre Robin Sequence & Micrognathia** — small mandible, glossoptosis (tongue falls back blocking airway), cleft palate, airway obstruction especially supine — Prone positioning helpful preoperatively; awake fiberoptic or LMA often necessary; avoid paralysis until airway secured; spontaneous ventilation preferred; consider retrograde intubation if fiberscope fails

## IX. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS BY PROCEDURE (8 topics)

71. **Otolaryngology & Airway Surgery** — shared airway with surgeon, fire risk (laser/cautery + high O2), microlaryngeal surgery (need for adequate ventilation in tiny space + hemostasis), obstruction risk (blood in airway, swelling), tubes/materials in surgical field — Jet ventilation or intermittent apnea techniques; fire safety protocol (low FiO2 <30%, moist gauge barriers, standby for intubation); careful positioning; post-op airway assessment essential

72. **Cardiac Surgery** — high perioperative risk, cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) implications (hemodilution, hypothermia, coagulopathy, reperfusion injury), monitoring (invasive hemodynamics, TEE), blood conservation — Balanced anesthesia technique; volatile agents in some protocols; opioid-heavy anesthesia for CABG; TEE placement before CPB; anticoagulation management (heparin, reversal); coagulopathy management post-CPB (platelets, FFP, cryoprecipitate, reversal agents)

73. **Neurosurgery** — cerebral protection (maintain CPP, avoid hypoxemia, prevent swelling), sitting position (VAE risk), prone position (airway access limited, prone positioning injury), increased ICP (avoid hypercapnia, hyperthermia, coughing), brain relaxation (mannitol, hypertonic saline, hyperventilation) — Awake craniotomy for motor/language mapping; neurophysiologic monitoring (MEP, SSEPs); arterial line standard; position-related complications (brachial plexus, pressure ulcers); emergence smooth (no coughing/straining)

74. **Vascular Surgery: Aortic Cross-Clamping** — ischemia distal to clamp (organ hypoperfusion), reperfusion injury (reactive oxygen species, inflammatory cascade), hyperkalemia on clamp removal, metabolic acidosis — Warn surgical team before clamp release; expect hypotension + hyperkalemia post-clamp; increase volume before reperfusion; calcium chloride, sodium bicarbonate, glucose + insulin for hyperkalemia; monitor closely for arrhythmias; epidural anesthesia useful for peripheral vascular cases

75. **Laparoscopic Surgery** — CO2 insufflation (hypercarbia, acidosis, systemic absorption), increased intra-abdominal pressure (ICP, reduced venous return, splanchnic ischemia), positioning (head-down Trendelenburg for pelvic cases), limited access if conversion needed — Hyperventilate to maintain normocarbia; monitor ETCO2 closely; avoid excessive insufflation pressures (use 8-12 mmHg); position-related complications (brachial plexus, rhabdomyolysis); prepare for open conversion

76. **Robotic-Assisted Surgery** — extended operative times (stress response, rhabdomyolysis), patient positioning (extreme Trendelenburg/reverse Trendelenburg, lateral decubitus), instrument collision risk, airway access limited after docking — Anticipate prolonged paralysis requirement; aggressive fluid management; monitor for position-related complications; communicate with surgical team about instrument movements near airway/monitoring; plan for emergency de-docking

77. **Trauma Surgery (Multiple Injuries)** — hypovolemic shock, urgent airway control, high-risk anatomy (penetrating chest/abdominal trauma, head injury, spinal injury, burns), damage control philosophy (minimize operative time, staged surgeries), massive transfusion protocols — RSI standard; consider ketamine for induction (maintains sympathetic tone); avoid excessive fluid prior to hemorrhage control (permissive hypotension); type & cross before induction; damage control approach (temporary packing, abbreviated surgery); coagulopathy prevention (balanced transfusion ratios, early reversal agents, avoid hypothermia)

78. **Cesarean Section** — pregnant physiology (aortocaval compression, difficult airway, increased aspiration risk), hemodynamic instability common (sympathectomy from neuraxial, blood loss), fetal well-being considerations, emergency vs. scheduled (urgency affects approach) — Spinal anesthesia preferred when time permits (faster onset than epidural); general anesthesia for emergency with failed neuraxial (RSI, modified induction agents); recognize ruptured vessel if profound hypotension; active management of third stage (oxytocin IM/IV, ergot, TXA); blood products immediately available

79. **Emergency Surgery on Full Stomach** — aspiration risk high, NPO status unknown, urgent operative need (cannot delay for fasting), RSI mandate, alternative airway management critical if intubation fails — Rapid sequence intubation protocol essential; cricoid pressure 10 N application; prepare for emergency airway (supraglottic airway, emergency surgical airway); suction catheter immediately available at intubation; post-operative NPO status for airway edema assessment

## X. PHARMACOKINETICS & PHARMACODYNAMICS (5 topics)

80. **Drug Distribution & Compartmental Models** — central compartment (highly perfused: brain, heart, kidney), rapid equilibration, peripheral compartment (muscle, fat), slow equilibration, context-sensitive half-time (time for drug level to fall 50% after infusion discontinuation) — Remifentanil context-independent (eliminated rapidly regardless of infusion duration); propofol context-dependent (prolonged effect after long infusions due to redistribution from peripheral compartment); implications for maintenance infusions + emergence timing

81. **Hepatic Metabolism** — Phase I (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis), Phase II (conjugation, glucuronidation), liver blood flow dependency (flow-limited extraction ratio high), drug interactions (enzyme induction/inhibition) — Chronic alcohol: enzyme induction → ↓ drug effect; acute liver failure: ↓ metabolism → ↑ drug effect; cirrhosis: reduced hepatic function + portal hypertension + coagulopathy; avoid hepatotoxic drugs (halothane fluoride ions, rare idiosyncratic reactions)

82. **Renal Elimination** — filtration (passive, dependent on GFR), secretion (active, saturable), reabsorption (passive), metabolite clearance (some active metabolites renally excreted) — Acute kidney injury/chronic kidney disease: ↓ GFR → drug accumulation; morphine metabolite M6G accumulates (neuroexcitatory); succinylcholine: pseudocholinesterase deficiency prolongs effect; avoid renally dependent drugs in renal failure (cisatracurium, atracurium preferred neuromuscular blockers)

83. **Drug Protein Binding & Drug Interactions** — highly protein-bound drugs (propofol 97%, bupivacaine 96%) compete for binding sites, albumin vs. alpha-1 acid glycoprotein binding, competition → ↑ free (active) drug — Hypoalbuminemia (liver disease, malnutrition, nephrotic syndrome): ↑ free drug effect; acidosis: ↓ protein binding → ↑ free drug; implications for dosing in critically ill patients; local anesthetic toxicity risk if bound drug suddenly released

84. **ED50 & Dose-Response Curves** — effective dose for 50% of population, steep vs. flat curves (narrow vs. wide therapeutic range), potency vs. efficacy distinction — Neuromuscular blockers: steep dose-response → small dose changes have big effect (use careful titration); volatile anesthetics: steep curve (MAC concept); understanding curve shape guides titration strategy; inter-individual variability in response (genetics, comorbidities, age)

## XI. FLUIDS, ELECTROLYTES & HEMODYNAMICS (6 topics)

85. **Intravenous Fluid Management** — maintenance fluids (2-4-2 rule for pediatrics, 1 ml/kg/hr for adults post-induction), third-space losses (peritoneal surface exposure × 0.5-1 ml/kg/hr), blood loss replacement (crystalloid 3-4:1 ratio, target urine output >0.5 ml/kg/hr adult), ongoing losses (diarrhea, nasogastric drainage, insensible losses) — Hyperchloremic acidosis risk with excessive 0.9% saline (preferred 4:1 saline + balanced crystalloid in high volume); avoid fluid overload in cardiac disease; fluid status reassessment with urine output + vital signs + CVP trend

86. **Crystalloid vs. Colloid Fluids** — crystalloid (lactated Ringer's, 0.9% saline, PlasmaLyte) widely distributed (3-4 L extracellular space for 1 L crystalloid), cost-effective, coagulopathy if excessive (dilution); colloid (albumin, dextran, hydroxyethyl starch) retained intravascular (1 L colloid ≈ 1 L plasma), expensive, HES thrombotic + renal toxicity, albumin reduced mortality in sepsis — Crystalloid standard for most cases; colloid not superior for survival; balanced crystalloid reduces hyperchloremic acidosis risk

87. **Hyperkalemia Management** — presentation (peaked T waves, widened QRS, bradycardia, cardiac arrest risk), intraoperative triggers (tissue trauma, succinylcholine, renal failure, blood product transfusion), treatment (calcium gluconate 10% 10-20 ml IV for cardiac membrane stabilization, insulin 10 units + dextrose 25g IV, albuterol 10-20 mg nebulized, sodium bicarbonate 50 mEq IV if acidosis) — Monitor ECG continuously when hyperkalemia suspected; check serum K+ immediately; avoid succinylcholine in renal failure/crush injury/burn patients; calcium stabilization first priority (protects heart)

88. **Hyponatremia & Hypernatremia** — hyponatremia (Na <130 mEq/L, symptomatic: seizure, altered mental status), hypernatremia (Na >150 mEq/L, insensible losses, inadequate free water), fluid restriction for hyponatremia (treat slowly to avoid central pontine myelinolysis), hypertonic saline (3%) for symptomatic hyponatremia or severe hypernatremia — Avoid rapid correction (risk demyelination); intraoperative causes (excessive hypotonic fluids, SIADH from pain/nausea, dilutional from large-volume crystalloid); serum osmolality calculation guides treatment

89. **Calcium & Magnesium Abnormalities** — hypocalcemia (muscle cramps, altered mental status, prolonged QT, tetany, cardiac arrhythmias), hypermagnesemia (muscle weakness, cardiac conduction abnormalities), hypomagnesemia (arrhythmia risk, exacerbates hypokalemia) — Citrate anticoagulation (massive transfusion) causes hypocalcemia; treatment calcium gluconate 10% 10-20 ml IV; check Mg levels in arrhythmia unresponsive to other therapies; magnesium 2g IV bolus for refractory hypomagnesemia

90. **Coagulopathy Management** — dilutional (hemodilution from fluid resuscitation, ROTEM/TEG monitoring if massive transfusion), consumption (DIC from tissue trauma, massive transfusion), acidosis/hypothermia impair coagulation, VTE prophylaxis (mechanical, pharmacologic timing) — Balanced transfusion ratios (1:1:1 PRBC:FFP:platelets in massive transfusion), reversal agents (idarucizumab for dabigatran, 4-factor PCC for warfarin/DOAC, tranexamic acid 1g IV bolus for bleeding + hyperfibrinolysis), monitor fibrinogen (maintain >100 mg/dl in massive transfusion), factor correction as indicated

## XII. COMPLICATIONS & SAFETY (5 topics)

91. **Postoperative Nausea & Vomiting (PONV)** — risk factors (female, non-smoker, history of PONV/motion sickness, opioid use, volatile anesthesia, inadequate anesthesia), prophylaxis (dexamethasone 8 mg, ondansetron 4 mg, aprepitant 40 mg), rescue (haloperidol 0.5-1 mg IV, droperidol 0.625-1.25 mg IV, scopolamine transdermal patch 1.5 mg) — TIVA (propofol/remifentanil) reduces PONV vs. volatile; avoid opioid-heavy anesthesia in high-risk patients; multimodal prophylaxis more effective than monotherapy; post-operative analgesia without excessive opioids

92. **Delayed Emergence & Awareness** — delayed emergence causes (residual anesthesia from prolonged infusion, hypothermia, hypoglycemia, residual neuromuscular blockade, hepatic/renal disease, ICU sedation continuation) — Awareness (conscious but paralyzed, terrifying experience, PTSD risk): prevention (adequate anesthesia, neuromuscular monitoring to prevent paralysis without anesthesia, BIS monitoring in high-risk cases); treatment (sedation + amnesia if recognized intraoperatively, post-operative counseling + psychological support); incidence 1-2 per 1000 in general population, higher in cardiac/trauma with rapid sequence

93. **Perioperative Myocardial Infarction & Cardiac Morbidity** — risk factors (CAD, age >70, diabetes, renal disease, vascular disease), perioperative NSTEMI (troponin elevation without ST changes, high-risk), prevention (beta-blockers, statins, tight glycemic control), monitoring (ECG, troponin serial), treatment (antiplatelets, anticoagulation, revascularization if appropriate) — Troponin elevation increases mortality; avoid tachycardia + hypertension; maintain diastolic BP >60 mmHg (critical for coronary perfusion); optimize oxygenation + hemoglobin

94. **Peripheral Nerve Injury** — mechanisms (pressure/stretching from positioning, needle/catheter trauma, local anesthetic toxicity, ischemia from tourniquet), risk factors (tight positioning, male gender, longer operations), prevention (padding, position changes, avoid aggressive retraction, monitor for paresthesia during block), recovery (usually weeks-months if not severe) — Most common: brachial plexus (positioning), ulnar nerve (elbow pressure), common peroneal (leg) — position assessment post-operatively; EMG/NCS if persistent; liability concern; documentation critical

95. **Aspiration Pneumonitis vs. Bacterial Pneumonia** — Mendelson syndrome (chemical pneumonitis from acid aspiration: hypoxemia within minutes, infiltrates on CXR, fever, bronchospasm), bacterial pneumonia (later presentation 12-48 hr, productive sputum, consolidation) — Prevention (NPO, H2 antagonist, proton pump inhibitor, metoclopramide, RSI); treatment (supportive care for Mendelson, avoid prophylactic antibiotics unless high-risk clinical scenario); prognosis (most recover with supportive care); document aspiration witnessed/suspected for medicolegal purposes

## XIII. PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE & PERIOPERATIVE MEDICINE (6 topics)

96. **Practice Management & Documentation** — pre-operative assessment documentation (physical status, airway assessment, medication allergies, plan, risks discussed, informed consent), intraoperative record (drugs + times, monitors, vital signs, events, hemodynamics), post-operative note (emergence quality, complications, follow-up plan) — Accurate records critical for medicolegal protection; contemporaneous documentation preferred; avoid alterations; communicate with surgical team + recovery room staff

97. **Medication Safety & Error Prevention** — high-alert medications (neuromuscular blockers look-alikes, concentrated potassium, insulin, opioids), labeling (all syringes labeled before administration), "challenge-response" communication protocol, standardized drug concentrations, double-checks for critical medications — Distraction management (minimize OR traffic during critical times), fatigue-related errors (schedule awareness, hand-offs), team-based safety culture; incident reporting without blame; near-miss discussion valuable for prevention

98. **Physician Impairment & Substance Abuse** — recognition of impaired colleague (tremor, odor of alcohol, poor judgment, deteriorating performance), reporting obligation (mandatory reporting in many states), intervention pathway (employee assistance programs, state physician health programs), recovery support — Anesthesiologists at higher risk (access to drugs, stress); early intervention improves outcomes; confidential reporting mechanisms protect both patients + impaired colleague; return-to-work protocols include monitoring + random drug screening

99. **Ethical Issues in Anesthesia** — informed consent (discuss risks/benefits/alternatives), autonomy (respect patient wishes, advance directives, do-not-resuscitate status), beneficence/non-maleficence (maximize benefit, minimize harm), justice (fair resource allocation), conflict of interest (avoid financial incentives affecting clinical decisions) — Difficult cases: refusal of blood products (Jehovah's Witnesses, alternatives: cell salvage, volume expansion, synthetic oxygen carriers); vulnerable populations (children, elderly, cognitively impaired); ethical consultation available for complex cases

100. **Quality Improvement & Outcomes Monitoring** — perioperative adverse events tracking (mortality, unplanned ICU admission, hospital-acquired infections, VTE, bleeding complications, PONV, pain control), benchmarking against national standards, root-cause analysis of serious events, implementation of system improvements — Safety culture (non-punitive error reporting, focus on systems not individuals), collaborative improvement projects (checklists, standardization, education), measurement of outcomes + process metrics for accountability; quality improvement essential for accreditation + credentialing
