## PHASE 1: FOUNDATION STABILIZATION (Weeks 1–4)

### Goal: Establish core diagnostic frameworks that unlock all downstream learning

#### Week 1: **H&P Methodology + Differential Diagnosis (2 modules)**

**Module 1: History & Physical Examination (10–12 hours)**
- Source: Washington Manual "History & Physical" chapter + UpToDate "Approach to Patient" series
- Content needed:
  - Chief complaint → differential generation (broad → narrow)
  - System-based H&P structure (ROS template, physical exam findings by organ system)
  - Key elements for 5 major presentations: chest pain, dyspnea, abdominal pain, altered mental status, fever
  - Red flag recognition (alarm symptoms triggering urgent action)
  - Admission order elements (admit vs. discharge criteria, workup prioritization)
- Expected units: 150–200 (chunked by system/presentation)
- High-yield facts: < 50 facts; emphasis on clinical pattern recognition
- Testing: Case-based scenarios (differential generation)

**Module 2: Systematic Differential Diagnosis by Presentation (8–10 hours)**
- Source: UpToDate "Clinical Approach to..." series (select 5 high-frequency presentations)
- Content needed (5 workflows):
  1. **Dyspnea:** Cardiopulmonary vs. other, acute vs. chronic decision tree
  2. **Chest Pain:** ACS vs. PE vs. pericarditis vs. other, troponin timing
  3. **Abdominal Pain:** RUQ vs. epigastric vs. RLQ vs. LLQ vs. diffuse, obstruction vs. inflammation
  4. **Altered Mental Status:** Delirium vs. dementia vs. intoxication vs. stroke vs. infection
  5. **Fever:** Infectious vs. inflammatory, timeline-based prioritization
- Expected units: 100–150
- Integration point: Link each differential to downstream diagnosis sections (pneumonia, ACS, etc.)

**Effort:** 20–25 hours (sourcing + chunking + linking to existing anesthesia content)
**Dependency:** None; unlocks all downstream learning
**Timeline:** Weeks 1–2

---

#### Week 2: **Evidence-Based Medicine (Basics) + Guideline Interpretation (8–10 hours)**

**Module 3: EBM Fundamentals + Guideline Strength (6–8 hours)**
- Source: Guyatt's "Users' Guides to Medical Literature" (BMJ series or textbook chapter)
- Content:
  - Study design hierarchy (RCT > observational > case series > expert opinion)
  - Power, p-value, confidence intervals (intuitive, not mathematical)
  - Guideline grading (GRADE: strong vs. weak recommendations, high/moderate/low quality evidence)
  - Applicability (patient population match to guideline cohort)
  - Metrics (NNT, NNH, absolute vs. relative risk reduction)
- Expected units: 80–100
- Integration: Map to specific clinical scenarios (antibiotic selection, HTN targets, etc.)
- High-yield: < 30 facts; focus on "when to trust a study" reasoning

**Effort:** 6–8 hours
**Timeline:** Week 2

---

#### Week 3: **Stroke (Acute, Ischemic & Hemorrhagic) — Time-Critical Emergency (8–10 hours)**

**Module 4: Acute Stroke Recognition & Management (8–10 hours)**
- Source: AHA/ASA 2024 Stroke Guidelines + UpToDate Acute Stroke
- Content:
  - NIHSS scoring + imaging interpretation (CT to exclude hemorrhage, MRI/CTA/CTP for ischemia)
  - Thrombolytic criteria (alteplase IV within 4.5 hr, patient selection)
  - Thrombectomy criteria (large vessel occlusion, up to 24 hr with imaging evidence)
  - Contraindications (INR >1.7, platelet <100k, recent surgery/bleed)
  - Door-to-needle/door-to-puncture time metrics
  - Post-stroke complications (hemorrhagic transformation, cerebral edema, aspiration)
  - Discharge criteria + outpatient follow-up (TIA vs. minor stroke vs. major stroke)
- Expected units: 120–150
- Factbase: < 50 facts (focus on decision points)
- Testing: Stroke alert scenarios (rapid triage)

**Effort:** 8–10 hours
**Timeline:** Week 3

---

#### Week 4: **Seizures + Meningitis (High-Mortality Emergencies) (12–14 hours)**

**Module 5: Seizure Recognition & Status Epilepticus (6–7 hours)**
- Source: UpToDate Seizure series + AES Guidelines
- Content:
  - Seizure vs. syncope vs. stroke vs. migraine distinction
  - Generalized vs. focal onset classification
  - Status epilepticus recognition + first-line management (benzodiazepines, lorazepam/midazolam dosing)
  - Second-line (phenytoin, levetiracetam, valproate)
  - EEG timing + basic interpretation (seizure activity, status epilepticus patterns)
  - Post-seizure workup (metabolic panel, imaging, EEG)
  - Seizure prophylaxis (head injury, stroke, TBI)
- Expected units: 80–100
- Factbase: < 40 facts

**Module 6: Meningitis (Bacterial, Viral, Fungal) (6–7 hours)**
- Source: CDC/IDSA Meningitis Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - Classic triad + atypical presentations (elderly, immunocompromised, early)
  - CSF analysis (cell counts, glucose, protein, Gram stain, culture, PCR)
  - Empiric antibiotics by age + risk factors:
    - Age <18: Ceftriaxone + vancomycin (+ ampicillin if <3 mo or >50 yr for Listeria)
    - Age 18–50: Ceftriaxone + vancomycin
    - Age >50: Ceftriaxone + vancomycin + ampicillin
  - Duration (10–14 days bacterial, 7–10 days viral)
  - Dexamethasone use (pre- or concurrent with first antibiotic)
  - Isolation duration (24 hr droplet precautions post-first dose)
  - Prophylaxis (contacts: ciprofloxacin/rifampin for meningococcal)
- Expected units: 80–100
- Factbase: < 50 facts

**Effort:** 12–14 hours
**Timeline:** Week 4

## PHASE 2: CRITICAL DIAGNOSIS EXPANSION (Weeks 5–12)

### Goal: Cover highest-frequency, highest-mortality diagnoses

#### **Week 5: Pneumonia + Antibiotic Stewardship (12–14 hours)**

**Module 7: CAP/HAP/VAP Diagnosis & Treatment (7–8 hours)**
- Source: IDSA/ATS 2023 CAP Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - CURB-65 + PSI score interpretation
  - Chest X-ray interpretation (lobar vs. interstitial, distribution)
  - Blood cultures + sputum culture timing
  - Empiric antibiotic selection by severity + risk factors:
    - Outpatient: Amoxicillin-clavulanate or macrolide
    - Hospitalized (non-ICU): Ceftriaxone ± macrolide OR fluoroquinolone
    - ICU: Cephalosporin + fluoroquinolone OR cephalosporin + macrolide
  - Atypical pathogens (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella)
  - HAP/VAP risk factors + empiric selection (pseudomonas coverage if risk factors)
  - Duration (5–7 days most; longer if complications)
  - Failure-to-improve management (repeat imaging, different organisms, complications)
- Expected units: 100–120
- Factbase: < 50 facts (focus on severity scoring + antibiotic selection)

**Module 8: Antibiotic Stewardship Framework (5–6 hours)**
- Source: IDSA Antibiotic Stewardship Program + Sanford Guide
- Content (decision tree for empiric selection):
  - Gram-negative coverage: 3rd gen cephalosporin vs. fluoroquinolone vs. carbapenem
    - E. coli (most common): 3rd gen ceph or FQ
    - Pseudomonas (if risk): Cephalosporin (ceftazidime) or carbapenem
    - ESBL (if risk): Carbapenem
  - Gram-positive coverage: Vancomycin vs. oxacillin vs. penicillin
    - S. aureus: Oxacillin (if susceptible) or vancomycin (if MRSA risk)
    - S. pneumoniae: Penicillin or cephalosporin
  - Anaerobes: Clindamycin vs. beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor
  - Common presentations:
    - Pneumonia: Cephalosporin ± macrolide
    - UTI: TMP-SMX, cephalosporin, or fluoroquinolone
    - Skin/soft tissue: Oxacillin or clindamycin (± vancomycin if MRSA)
    - Abdominal: Cephalosporin + metronidazole or carbapenems
  - De-escalation principles (narrow once culture/susceptibility known)
  - Duration by diagnosis (5–7 days most common)
  - C. difficile risk with broad-spectrum agents
- Expected units: 80–100
- Factbase: < 40 facts (structured decision trees)

**Effort:** 12–14 hours
**Timeline:** Week 5

---

#### **Week 6: Valvular Heart Disease + CAD/ACS (15–17 hours)**

**Module 9: Valvular Heart Disease (8–10 hours)**
- Source: Cardiology UpToDate + AHA Valvular Disease Guidelines
- Content:
  - Aortic stenosis: Pathophysiology (calcification, rheumatic), symptoms (angina, syncope, DOE), echo findings (AVA, mean gradient), perioperative risk, outcome (disease progression 2–3 yr to symptoms)
  - Aortic regurgitation: Acute vs. chronic (volume overload, wide pulse pressure), hemodynamic consequences, surgical indications (EF <50%)
  - Mitral stenosis: Rheumatic etiology, A-fib stroke risk, echo findings (MV area, LA enlargement), perioperative risk, surgical indications
  - Mitral regurgitation: Primary (degenerative, endocarditis) vs. secondary (functional), surgical indications, LV dilation progression
  - Prosthetic valves: Mechanical (warfarin requirement, thrombotic risk) vs. bioprosthetic (degeneration timeline)
  - Perioperative risk stratification (asymptomatic vs. symptomatic, degree of stenosis/regurgitation)
  - Echocardiography basics: Valve area, gradients, chamber dimensions, ejection fraction
  - Endocarditis risk (mitral prolapse, aortic regurgitation, prosthetic valve)
- Expected units: 120–150
- Factbase: < 60 facts (echo parameters + decision points)

**Module 10: CAD/ACS Diagnosis & Management (7–8 hours)**
- Source: AHA/ACC 2023 ACS Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - Anginal chest pain features (substernal, exertional, relieved by nitrates/rest)
  - Troponin kinetics (rises 2–4 hr, peaks 24–48 hr, clears 10–14 days)
  - High-sensitivity troponin use (more sensitive, slightly less specific)
  - TIMI/GRACE risk scores for prognosis + revascularization decision
  - EKG interpretation for STEMI (ST elevation + LBBB equivalent)
  - STEMI management: PCI within 120 min (primary) or thrombolytics if PCI not available
  - NSTEMI/unstable angina: Dual antiplatelet (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor: clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor), anticoagulation (UFH or LMWH), beta-blocker, statin
  - High-risk features (dynamic ECG changes, elevated troponin, hemodynamic instability)
  - Post-MI complications: Cardiogenic shock, papillary muscle rupture, ventricular septal defect, pericarditis
  - Dual antiplatelet duration (12 mo typical; shorter in high-bleed risk)
- Expected units: 100–120
- Factbase: < 50 facts (risk scores + treatment algorithms)

**Effort:** 15–17 hours
**Timeline:** Week 6

#### **Week 7: Pulmonary Emergencies (COPD, Asthma, PE) (14–16 hours)**

**Module 11: COPD Exacerbation (6–7 hours)**
- Source: GOLD 2024 Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - COPD staging (GOLD: based on spirometry + symptoms)
  - Exacerbation triggers: Infection (bacterial, viral), air pollution, nonadherence
  - Spirometry interpretation (FEV1/FVC <0.70 = obstruction, FEV1 severity)
  - Acute management:
    - Oxygen titration (target SaO2 88–92% to avoid CO2 retention, hypercapnia)
    - Bronchodilators: Albuterol + ipratropium (anticholinergic)
    - Corticosteroids: Oral prednisone 40–50 mg daily × 5 days (or IV methylprednisolone)
    - Antibiotics: If purulent sputum + productive cough (amoxicillin-clavulanate, macrolide, or fluoroquinolone)
  - NIV (non-invasive ventilation) indications: pH <7.35, PaCO2 >45 + altered mental status
  - ICU admission criteria: Severe dyspnea, altered mental status, severe hypoxemia/hypercapnia, inability to protect airway
  - Discharge criteria: Stable oxygen requirement, able to walk, cough productive but manageable, access to outpatient care
- Expected units: 80–100
- Factbase: < 40 facts (FEV1 thresholds, drug dosing)

**Module 12: Asthma Exacerbation (4–6 hours)**
- Source: NAEPP Asthma Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - Asthma phenotypes (allergic, non-allergic, occupational, AERD)
  - FEV1 interpretation (% predicted, FEV1/FVC)
  - Exacerbation severity:
    - Mild-moderate: FEV1 50–80%, tachypnea, normal speech
    - Severe: FEV1 30–50%, can only say few words, diaphoresis
    - Status asthmaticus: FEV1 <30%, silent chest, altered mental status (CO2 retention)
  - Acute management:
    - Oxygen (target SaO2 >90%)
    - Beta-2 agonist: Albuterol 2.5–5 mg nebulized every 20 min × 3 doses, then every 1–4 hr
    - Corticosteroids: Prednisone 40–50 mg or methylprednisolone 125 mg IV every 6 hr
    - Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV if severe (bronchodilator effect)
    - ICU admission: Severe exacerbation, altered mental status, severe hypoxemia, FEV1 <25%, pH <7.35
  - Discharge criteria: FEV1 >50% predicted, sustained improvement 1+ hr post-nebulizer, adequate outpatient access
- Expected units: 60–80
- Factbase: < 30 facts (severity criteria, drug dosing)

**Module 13: PE/DVT Diagnosis & Management (6–7 hours)**
- Source: CHEST Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - PE risk factors (immobility >4 days, surgery, malignancy, hypercoagulability, pregnancy)
  - Wells score PE probability (scores 0–4 points; <2 = low risk)
  - D-dimer use (high sensitivity but low specificity; use only in low-clinical-probability)
  - Imaging by risk + D-dimer:
    - High clinical suspicion: Proceed directly to imaging (CTA PE protocol, V/Q if renal contraindication)
    - Moderate risk: D-dimer; if positive → CTA
    - Low risk: D-dimer; if negative → reassure; if positive → CTA
  - RV dysfunction assessment (echo, BNP, troponin) for risk stratification
  - Anticoagulation:
    - Initial: UFH bolus + infusion OR LMWH (enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h)
    - Switch to warfarin (target INR 2–3) or DOAC after 5–7 days parenteral
  - Thrombolysis indications: Massive PE with hemodynamic instability
  - IVC filter indications: Contraindication to anticoagulation, recurrent PE on anticoagulation, massive PE with RV failure
  - Duration: Provoked PE (surgery, trauma) = 3 months; unprovoked = minimum 3 months (consider extended if recurrent, malignancy, thrombophilia)
  - DVT: Similar anticoagulation; compression stocking evidence weak; early mobilization priority
- Expected units: 100–120
- Factbase: < 50 facts (risk scores, imaging interpretation, anticoagulation algorithms)

**Effort:** 14–16 hours
**Timeline:** Week 7

#### **Weeks 8–9: Renal & Electrolyte Expansion + GI Bleeding (12–14 hours)**

**Module 14: AKI/CKD Differential & Management (6–8 hours)**
- Source: Nephrology UpToDate
- Content:
  - AKI staging (KDIGO: creatinine rise + urine output)
  - Prerenal (hypoperfusion): FENa <1%, urine osmolality >500, improvement with fluids
  - Intrinsic (ATN, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis): FENa >2%, muddy casts, kidney biopsy if indicated
  - Postrenal (obstruction): Bladder distension, hydronephrosis on ultrasound
  - CKD staging (GFR-based: stage 1–5, stage 5 = <15 mL/min)
  - Medications in renal failure (avoid renally-dependent, adjust dosing)
  - Hyperkalemia management in AKI (calcium gluconate, insulin + dextrose, albuterol, sodium bicarbonate)
  - Dialysis indications (pH <7.15, K >6.5 unresponsive, fluid overload, uremia with altered mental status)
- Expected units: 80–100
- Factbase: < 40 facts (staging, FENa cutoffs, medication adjustments)

**Module 15: GI Bleeding — Upper & Lower (8–10 hours)**
- Source: ASGE Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - Upper GI bleeding etiologies:
    - Peptic ulcer disease (most common): H. pylori + NSAIDs
    - Variceal: Esophageal varices (cirrhosis/portal hypertension)
    - Mallory-Weiss tear: Retching, alcohol use
    - Boerhaave syndrome (rupture): Severe retching, mediastinitis risk
  - Lower GI bleeding etiologies:
    - Hemorrhoids (most common): Blood on toilet paper
    - Diverticulosis: Painless bleeding, older age
    - IBD (ulcerative colitis, Crohn's): Diarrhea + blood
    - Angiodysplasia: Elderly, chronic kidney disease
  - Hemodynamic assessment: Orthostatic vitals, mental status, urine output
  - Initial management:
    - IV access (2 large-bore IVs), type & cross, hold NPO
    - Crystalloid resuscitation (1–2 L bolus), target MAP >65
    - Correction of coagulopathy (warfarin reversal, platelets if <50k)
    - RBC transfusion threshold: Hgb <7 (or <9 if cardiac disease)
  - Transfusion targets:
    - Non-variceal: NPO + PPI (high-dose IV omeprazole, infusion)
    - Variceal: PPI + octreotide + endoscopic therapy + prophylactic antibiotics (ceftriaxone 1 g daily)
  - Endoscopy timing:
    - Emergent (within 2 hr): Unstable or massive bleeding
    - Urgent (within 24 hr): Most bleeds
  - Repeat bleeding management: Repeat endoscopy, interventional radiology (IR), surgery if refractory
- Expected units: 120–150
- Factbase: < 50 facts (etiology + transfusion thresholds)

**Effort:** 12–14 hours
**Timeline:** Weeks 8–9

#### **Weeks 10–12: Endocrinology Expansion (16–20 hours)**

**Module 16: Diabetes (Type 1 & 2): Diagnosis, Classification, Chronic Management (10–12 hours)**
- Source: ADA Standards of Medical Care + UpToDate
- Content:
  - Diagnostic criteria:
    - Fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL (8 hr fasting)
    - 2-hr glucose ≥200 mg/dL on oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)
    - A1C ≥6.5%
    - Random glucose ≥200 mg/dL + symptoms
  - Type 1: Autoimmune, beta-cell destruction, insulin requirement, peak age teen–30s
  - Type 2: Insulin resistance + beta-cell dysfunction, slow onset, risk factors (obesity, family history, age)
  - Gestational: Diagnosed in pregnancy, resolves postpartum (high T2DM risk later)
  - Oral agents:
    - Metformin (first-line): Decreases hepatic glucose, weight neutral, lactic acidosis risk in renal failure
    - Sulfonylureas (glyburide, glipizide): Insulin secretion, hypoglycemia risk, weight gain
    - DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin): GLP-1 enhancement, neutral on weight/glucose
    - GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide): Weight loss, cardiovascular benefit, GI side effects
    - SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin): Cardiovascular + renal protective, DKA risk, UTI risk
    - Thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone): Insulin sensitivity, weight gain, heart failure risk
  - Insulin types: Rapid-acting (lispro, aspart), short-acting (regular), intermediate (NPH), long-acting (glargine, degludec), basal-bolus regimens
  - Glucose targets by age/comorbidity:
    - Non-elderly: Fasting 80–130, A1C <7%
    - Older adults: Less stringent (A1C 7–8%), avoid hypoglycemia
    - Pregnancy: Tighter control (fasting <95, 1-hr post-meal <140)
  - Hypoglycemia: Symptoms (tremor, palpitations, sweating), glucose <70 mg/dL, treatment (15 g fast carbs), unawareness in long-standing diabetes
  - Monitoring: A1C every 3 mo (glycemic control), annual foot exam, annual ophthalmology, annual kidney function (creatinine, urine albumin)
- Expected units: 150–180
- Factbase: < 60 facts (diagnostic criteria, drug classes, targets)

**Module 17: Thyroid Disease (Hypothyroidism, Hyperthyroidism, Thyroid Storm) (6–8 hours)**
- Source: AACE Thyroid Guidelines + UpToDate
- Content:
  - TSH/free T4 interpretation (TSH primary screening; free T4 if abnormal)
  - Primary hypothyroidism (elevated TSH, low free T4): Auto-immune (Hashimoto's) most common, iodine deficiency (worldwide), medications (amiodarone, lithium, PTU)
  - Secondary hypothyroidism (low TSH, low free T4): Central cause (pituitary, hypothalamus)
  - Levothyroxine replacement: Starting dose 25–50 mcg, titrate by 25 mcg every 6–8 weeks, target TSH 0.5–5.0 (or 0.1–0.5 if post-thyroid cancer)
  - Drug interactions (calcium, iron, PPIs decrease absorption; wait 4 hr after levothyroxine)
  - Primary hyperthyroidism (low TSH, high free T4): Graves disease (antibodies, diffuse goiter), toxic nodule (single autonomously-secreting nodule), thyroiditis (self-limited inflammation)
  - Antithyroid medications:
    - PTU (propylthiouracil): Inhibits TPO + iodine incorporation + peripheral T4→T3 conversion, agranulocytosis risk (0.1–0.2%)
    - Methimazole: Inhibits TPO + iodine, faster action, PTU preferred in first trimester (PTU has hepatotoxicity)
  - Beta-blockers for symptom relief (propranolol also blocks peripheral conversion)
  - Thyroid storm: Temperature >38.5°C, altered mental status, hemodynamic instability (tachycardia, hypertension), precipitants (infection, surgery, medication non-compliance)
    - Management: PTU 150–200 mg PO/NG q4–6h (blocks hormone release + conversion), iodine solution (Lugol's or SSKI) 10 min after PTU (blocks hormone release), beta-blocker (propranolol), supportive care (IV fluids, cooling)
  - Testing before treatment: TSH receptor antibodies (Graves vs. nodule), radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU) helps classify
- Expected units: 100–120
- Factbase: < 50 facts (TSH/free T4 interpretation, drug dosing, storm management)

**Module 18: Adrenal Insufficiency (4–6 hours)**
- Source: Endocrinology UpToDate + AACE Guidelines
- Content:
  - Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease): Low cortisol, elevated ACTH, hyperpigmentation, hypotension, hyponatremia
  - Causes: Autoimmune (90% in US), TB (worldwide most common), fungal, adrenal hemorrhage, adrenoleukodystrophy
  - Secondary adrenal insufficiency: Low cortisol, low/normal ACTH, exogenous steroid withdrawal most common
  - ACTH stimulation test: Cosyntropin 250 mcg IV, measure cortisol at baseline + 30 min (normal >18 mcg/dL at 30 min)
  - Steroid replacement:
    - Hydrocortisone 15–20 mg daily (morning 10 mg, afternoon 5–10 mg) for primary insufficiency
    - Mineralocorticoid (fludrocortisone 0.1 mg daily) if primary
    - Increase dose during stress (surgery, infection, severe illness): double to triple usual dose
  - Acute adrenal crisis: Hypotension, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, altered mental status
    - Management: Hydrocortisone 50–100 mg IV q6–8h initially, rapid saline bolus, glucose
  - Patient education: Steroid card, dose increase during stress/illness
- Expected units: 60–80
- Factbase: < 30 facts (ACTH stim interpretation, steroid dosing)

**Effort:** 16–20 hours
**Timeline:** Weeks 10–12
