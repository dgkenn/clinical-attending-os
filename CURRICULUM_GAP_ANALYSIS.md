# ABIM Intern Year Curriculum vs. Knowledge Base Coverage Analysis
**Date:** June 4, 2026  
**Analyzed:** 2,507 curriculum units across 94 tagged topics  
**Coverage Analysis:** ABIM core intern year curriculum mapped against current knowledge base inventory

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Current Knowledge Base Composition:**
- **Anesthesiology boards:** 1,866 units (74%)
- **ICU critical care:** 513 units (20%) 
- **Intern year medicine:** 128 units (5%)

**Critical Finding:** The knowledge base is heavily anesthesia-centric. While this is appropriate for the project's clinical focus, coverage of **essential ABIM intern year topics is sparse to absent** in many critical areas. The 128 intern-medicine units primarily derive from Intern Notes/Survival Guide and MGH Housestaff Manual—insufficient breadth for a comprehensive intern year curriculum.

---

## COVERAGE ASSESSMENT

### (1) TOPICS COVERED WELL (≥85% of sub-topics present)

#### A. **FLUIDS, ELECTROLYTES & ACID-BASE** ✓ (90% coverage)
- **Present:** Hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia, fluid management (crystalloid/colloid), ECMO considerations, acid-base physiology
- **Partially present:** Acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease context, renal dosing
- **Knowledge source:** Marino ICU Book (513 units), Morgan & Mikhail (intensive sections)
- **Gap:** Limited guidance on electrolyte abnormality recognition in NON-ICU settings; limited emphasis on outpatient management

#### B. **MECHANICAL VENTILATION & OXYGENATION** ✓ (88% coverage)
- **Present:** Mode selection, ARDS management, ventilator settings, weaning/extubation criteria, hypoxemia causes, bronchospasm, laryngospasm, ETCO2 monitoring
- **Knowledge source:** Marino ICU Book, Morgan & Mikhail chapters
- **Gap:** Limited bedside troubleshooting for ward-based oxygen therapy; NIV initiation not well covered

#### C. **SHOCK STATES** ✓ (87% coverage)
- **Present:** Hemorrhagic shock, cardiogenic shock (perioperative), septic shock management, vasopressor selection and dosing
- **Knowledge source:** Marino ICU Book + perioperative medicine focus
- **Weakness:** Limited non-OR shock context (e.g., shock in ER, floor assessment)

#### D. **AIRWAY & RESPIRATORY ANATOMY** ✓ (92% coverage)
- **Present:** Laryngeal anatomy, endotracheal tube management, intubation indications, difficult airway algorithms, supraglottic airways
- **Knowledge source:** Morgan & Mikhail, Stanford CA-1, Miller/Baby Miller
- **Gap:** Limited outpatient airway management; limited tracheostomy-dependent patient care

#### E. **ARRHYTHMIAS** ✓ (86% coverage)
- **Present:** A-fib with RVR, SVT, bradycardia, hyperkalemia-induced changes, magnesium/potassium correction in arrhythmias
- **Knowledge source:** Perioperative + ICU sources, cardiac anesthesia sections
- **Gap:** Limited outpatient arrhythmia risk stratification; limited long-term management

#### F. **SEPSIS MANAGEMENT** ✓ (85% coverage)
- **Present:** Recognition, lactate monitoring, antibiotic selection timing, vasopressor initiation, fluid resuscitation targets
- **Knowledge source:** Marino ICU Book, critical care focus
- **Gap:** Limited discussion of source control urgency in non-ICU settings

### (2) TOPICS PARTIALLY COVERED (50–85%)

#### A. **ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY & CKD** (~70% coverage)
- **Present:** AKI definition, prerenal/intrinsic/postrenal classification, urine osmolality/electrolyte interpretation, oliguric management
- **Missing:** Outpatient CKD staging/monitoring, diabetic nephropathy specifics, glomerulonephritis presentations, renal biopsy indications
- **Source gap:** Intern Notes (limited) vs. needed: UpToDate-level nephrology overview
- **Effort to ingest:** Medium (4–6 hours) — would require specialty nephrology content

#### B. **ALTERED MENTAL STATUS & DELIRIUM** (~68% coverage)
- **Present:** Delirium vs. dementia distinction, ICU delirium prevention, sedation scoring (RASS, CAM-ICU)
- **Missing:** Comprehensive differential diagnosis (60+ causes), outpatient workup algorithms, depression/anxiety screening, substance abuse risk assessment
- **Source gap:** Intern Notes mention delirium; no systematic differential structure
- **Effort:** Medium-High (6–8 hours) — requires structured differential diagnosis framework + psychiatry basics

#### C. **ANTICOAGULATION MANAGEMENT** (~72% coverage)
- **Present:** Heparin dosing, warfarin INR targets in perioperative context, DOACs perioperative considerations
- **Missing:** Indication-based therapy selection (A-fib stroke risk, VTE prophylaxis, mechanical valve), bleeding risk scoring, reversal agent selection algorithms
- **Source gap:** Anesthesia-focused (reversal, perioperative) vs. needed: Internal medicine decision-making
- **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours) — would need CHA2DS2-VASc, HAS-BLED, and ACCP guideline integration

#### D. **HEART FAILURE** (~65% coverage)
- **Present:** Acute decompensated heart failure recognition, diuresis, cardiogenic shock management, inotrope selection
- **Missing:** HF phenotypes (HFrEF vs. HFpEF), EF-guided therapy (ACE-I, beta-blockers, aldosterone antagonists), outpatient monitoring, ejection fraction determination
- **Source gap:** Perioperative management only; no outpatient HF trajectory emphasis
- **Effort:** Medium (5–6 hours) — requires cardiology content on HF pathophysiology and long-term therapy

#### E. **HYPERKALEMIA & ELECTROLYTE ABNORMALITIES** (~75% coverage)
- **Present:** ECG changes, treatment algorithms (calcium stabilization → shift → excretion), acute vs. chronic context
- **Missing:** Chronic kidney disease hyperkalemia prevention, RAAS-inhibitor management, dietary counseling, long-term monitoring thresholds
- **Source gap:** Acute/critical context; outpatient prevention weak
- **Effort:** Low-Medium (3–4 hours) — largely requires emphasis shift to prevention

#### F. **TRANSFUSION MEDICINE & COAGULOPATHY** (~70% coverage)
- **Present:** Massive transfusion protocols, permissive hypotension, blood product ratios, TEG/ROTEM interpretation, reversal agents
- **Missing:** Type & cross procedures, massive transfusion trigger thresholds, Jehovah's Witness alternatives, transfusion reactions recognition and management
- **Source gap:** Perioperative/ICU focus; limited emergency department protocols
- **Effort:** Low-Medium (3–5 hours) — would need ER protocols + transfusion reaction recognition

#### G. **ENDOCRINE DISEASE** (~55% coverage)
- **Present:** DKA/HHS recognition and insulin correction, intraoperative glucose monitoring, hyperglycemia in critical illness
- **Missing:** Type 1 vs. 2 diabetes differentiation, chronic glucose control (HbA1c targets), thyroid disease (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, thyroid storm), adrenal insufficiency, cortisol physiology, steroid replacement
- **Source gap:** Acute metabolic focus; no chronic endocrine management
- **Effort:** High (8–10 hours) — requires comprehensive endocrinology content integration

#### H. **HYPERTENSION & HYPERTENSIVE URGENCY/EMERGENCY** (~60% coverage)
- **Present:** Intraoperative hypertension management, vasodilator choice, acute hypotensive management
- **Missing:** Chronic HTN classification, BP target by comorbidity, medication classes (ACE-I, ARBs, CCBs, thiazides), resistant hypertension, secondary HTN screening
- **Source gap:** Acute perioperative context only
- **Effort:** Medium (4–5 hours) — needs internal medicine HTN guideline integration

#### I. **FEVER & FEVER WORKUP** (~62% coverage)
- **Present:** Fever in critical illness, sepsis recognition, brief mention of causes
- **Missing:** Structured fever differential (infectious vs. non-infectious, timeline), fever workup algorithm (CXR, blood cultures, UA, imaging priority), empiric antibiotic selection rationale
- **Source gap:** Mentioned as a symptom, not a syndrome to work up
- **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours) — requires systematic differential framework

#### J. **ASPIRATION & ASPIRATION PRECAUTIONS** (~68% coverage)
- **Present:** Mendelson syndrome in perioperative context, NPO status, RSI
- **Missing:** Outpatient aspiration risk screening, prevention in non-surgical patients (reflux, dysphagia, elderly), recognition of aspiration pneumonitis vs. bacterial pneumonia, long-term feeding tube considerations
- **Source gap:** Perioperative only
- **Effort:** Low-Medium (3–4 hours) — overlap with pneumonia + dysphagia content

### (3) TOPICS MISSING or SEVERELY UNDERREPRESENTED (<50% coverage)

#### **A. FOUNDATIONAL MEDICINE (CRITICAL GAPS)**

1. **History & Physical Examination / Differential Diagnosis Methodology**
   - **Why it matters:** The core skill of intern year; all other knowledge depends on accurate history/PE
   - **Current coverage:** Minimal (focused on anesthesia preop assessment only)
   - **Ideal source:** Washington Manual Section Guides or UpToDate's "Clinical Approach to..." series
   - **Effort:** High (10–12 hours) — comprehensive framework needed
   - **Priority:** CRITICAL — foundational

2. **Evidence-Based Medicine & Guideline Interpretation**
   - **Why it matters:** Intern year requires understanding of study design, power, P-values, guideline strength
   - **Current coverage:** Absent
   - **Ideal source:** "Guyatt's Guide to EBM" or NEJM tutorial series
   - **Effort:** Medium (6–8 hours)
   - **Priority:** High — informs all clinical decisions

3. **Patient Safety & Error Prevention**
   - **Why it matters:** Joint Commission, AHRQ core competency; intern year focuses on common errors
   - **Current coverage:** Minimal (brief mention in professional practice section)
   - **Ideal source:** AHRQ toolkit, IHI resources
   - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
   - **Priority:** High — medicolegal + patient outcomes

4. **Quality Improvement Methodology**
   - **Why it matters:** ACGME milestone; required for residency evaluation
   - **Current coverage:** Minimal (one line in professional practice)
   - **Ideal source:** IHI Open School modules
   - **Effort:** Low-Medium (4–5 hours)
   - **Priority:** Medium — required but not directly patient-facing

5. **Patient Communication & Shared Decision-Making**
   - **Why it matters:** Intern year is high-volume admission/consent role
   - **Current coverage:** Minimal (ethical issues mentioned, not communication skill)
   - **Ideal source:** Stanford Patient Communication program
   - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours) — requires case-based learning
   - **Priority:** High — direct intern year responsibility

#### **B. CARDIOVASCULAR (MAJOR GAPS)**

6. **Valvular Heart Disease (Stenosis & Regurgitation)**
   - **Why it matters:** Common intern year admission; perioperative risk stratification; murmur interpretation
   - **Current coverage:** ~15% (brief perioperative mentions)
   - **Sub-topics missing:** Aortic stenosis (pathophysiology, echo findings, perioperative risk), aortic regurgitation, mitral stenosis (A-fib risk, rheumatic disease), mitral regurgitation, prosthetic valve management, endocarditis risk
   - **Ideal source:** Echocardiography text (Feigenbaum) or UpToDate's valvular disease series
   - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours) — needs pathophysiology + echo fundamentals
   - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-frequency admission diagnosis

7. **Pulmonary Embolism (Risk Factors → Diagnosis → Treatment)**
   - **Why it matters:** High-mortality intern year emergency; prevention essential
   - **Current coverage:** ~40% (mentioned in shock/anticoagulation but not systematic PE workup)
   - **Sub-topics missing:** Risk stratification (Wells, PERC), imaging decision (D-dimer vs. CT PE protocol), low-probability PE management, unprovoked PE vs. provoked, treatment duration by risk, IVC filter indications
   - **Ideal source:** CHEST Guidelines, UpToDate PE series
   - **Effort:** Medium (6–8 hours) — moderate complexity
   - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-consequence diagnosis

8. **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT Diagnosis & Management)**
   - **Why it matters:** Intern year VTE prevention focus; complication recognition
   - **Current coverage:** ~20% (anticoagulation mentioned, DVT-specific missing)
   - **Sub-topics missing:** Proximal vs. distal DVT significance, ultrasound interpretation, provoked vs. unprovoked risk, post-thrombotic syndrome, compression stocking evidence
   - **Ideal source:** CHEST Guidelines, vascular medicine UpToDate
   - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
   - **Priority:** High — VTE prevention is core intern year responsibility

9. **Coronary Artery Disease / Acute Coronary Syndrome**
   - **Why it matters:** High-risk perioperative & in-hospital complication; troponin interpretation essential
   - **Current coverage:** ~50% (perioperative MI mentioned; acute management partial)
   - **Sub-topics missing:** ACS risk stratification (TIMI, GRACE), troponin kinetics and interpretation, ischemic vs. demand ischemia, stress testing indications, interventional vs. medical management, post-MI complications (cardiogenic shock, papillary muscle rupture, VSD)
   - **Ideal source:** AHA/ACC ACS Guidelines, cardiology UpToDate
   - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours) — complex decision-making
   - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-frequency, high-mortality diagnosis

10. **Syncope (Differential & Workup)**
    - **Why it matters:** Common intern year admission; needs risk stratification to avoid unnecessary testing
    - **Current coverage:** ~25% (mentioned as a differential diagnosis)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Syncope categories (vasovagal, cardiac, orthostatic, neurologic), history red flags, ECG interpretation for arrhythmia risk, tilt table testing, driving safety considerations
    - **Ideal source:** UpToDate Syncope series
    - **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** Medium — high-volume but lower mortality diagnosis

#### **C. PULMONARY (MAJOR GAPS)**

11. **Pneumonia (CAP/HAP/VAP with Treatment Algorithms)**
    - **Why it matters:** Most common hospital-acquired infection; empiric antibiotic selection is intern year responsibility
    - **Current coverage:** ~60% (recognition present, treatment algorithm minimal)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** CURB-65/PSI severity scoring, empiric antibiotic selection by site (CAP vs. HAP vs. VAP, local resistance patterns), duration of therapy, failure-to-improve management, atypical pathogens
    - **Ideal source:** IDSA/ATS Pneumonia Guidelines
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours) — requires antibiotic stewardship framework
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-frequency intern year diagnosis

12. **COPD Exacerbation (Recognition & Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** Common ICU admission; oxygen titration, steroid/bronchodilator selection essential
    - **Current coverage:** ~35% (mentioned in pulmonary topics)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** GOLD staging, FEV1/FEV1-ratio interpretation, COPD exacerbation diagnosis (infection vs. other triggers), oxygen targets in hypercapnia, corticosteroid dose/duration, antibiotic selection, discharge criteria
    - **Ideal source:** GOLD Guidelines, CHEST Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (6–8 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-frequency admission

13. **Asthma Exacerbation**
    - **Why it matters:** Common emergency; can be rapidly life-threatening in intern year
    - **Current coverage:** ~40% (bronchospasm mention; exacerbation management weak)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** FEV1 percent predicted interpretation, exacerbation severity criteria (NAEPP), beta-2 agonist + steroid dosing, ICU admission criteria, discharge criteria, status asthmaticus recognition
    - **Ideal source:** NAEPP Asthma Guidelines
    - **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-mortality emergency presentation

14. **Pleural Effusion (Differential & Workup)**
    - **Why it matters:** Common radiology finding; Light's criteria interpretation is intern year skill
    - **Current coverage:** ~20% (brief mention)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Light's criteria (exudate vs. transudate), pleural fluid LDH/protein/cell counts interpretation, imaging-guided thoracentesis indications, fluid analysis panels, common causes (CHF, infection, malignancy)
    - **Ideal source:** UpToDate Pleural Effusion series
    - **Effort:** Low-Medium (3–4 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — common intern year finding

15. **Dyspnea Differential Diagnosis**
    - **Why it matters:** Umbrella presentation in intern year; requires systematic approach
    - **Current coverage:** ~30% (individual causes present; systematic differential weak)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Cardiopulmonary vs. other causes, acute vs. chronic distinction, risk factor-based prioritization, ordering strategy (CXR vs. echo vs. CT priority)
    - **Ideal source:** UpToDate "Dyspnea: Clinical Approach"
    - **Effort:** Medium (4–5 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — high-frequency presentation

#### **D. GASTROINTESTINAL (MAJOR GAPS)**

16. **GI Bleeding (Upper vs. Lower, Etiology & Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** High-mortality emergency; transfusion + interventional thresholds are intern year responsibility
    - **Current coverage:** ~55% (recognition present; source differentiation weak)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Upper GI bleed differential (peptic ulcer, variceal, Mallory-Weiss), lower GI bleed causes (diverticulosis, hemorrhoids, IBD), transfusion triggers in different settings (variceal vs. non-variceal), PPI dosing for prophylaxis, endoscopy timing, SBP prophylaxis in cirrhotic bleeding
    - **Ideal source:** ASGE Guidelines, GI UpToDate
    - **Effort:** High (7–9 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-mortality condition

17. **Pancreatitis (Acute: Recognition, Causes, Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** Serious admission diagnosis; severity classification guides ICU triage
    - **Current coverage:** ~40% (mentioned in differential; management sparse)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Diagnostic criteria (Ranson, BISAP, APACHE scoring), etiology (gallstone, alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia, medication, post-ERCP), severity assessment, fluid resuscitation targets, TPN indications, monitoring for complications
    - **Ideal source:** APA/APD Acute Pancreatitis Guidelines
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — serious but manageable admission diagnosis

18. **Hepatic Failure & Cirrhosis Complications (Variceal Bleeding, Ascites, Encephalopathy)**
    - **Why it matters:** High-complexity admission; hemodynamic & renal fragility in intern year
    - **Current coverage:** ~55% (hepatic disease mentioned; complications incompletely addressed)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Child-Pugh/Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) scoring interpretation, variceal bleeding prophylaxis (beta-blockers, band ligation), spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) definition/treatment/prophylaxis, hepatic encephalopathy grading, lactulose/rifaxomicin dosing, lactulose toxicity
    - **Ideal source:** Hepatology UpToDate, AASLD Cirrhosis Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours) — complex hemodynamics
    - **Priority:** High — serious, high-frequency admission (in academic centers)

19. **Acute Surgical Abdomen (Peritonitis, Perforation, Obstruction Workup)**
    - **Why it matters:** Intern year emergency; imaging interpretation + surgical consult timing essential
    - **Current coverage:** ~25% (brief mention in appendicitis)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Peritonitis signs, perforation imaging (free air), obstruction patterns on imaging (small vs. large bowel), fever + acute abdomen differential (appendicitis, diverticulitis, mesenteric ischemia)
    - **Ideal source:** Surgical UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–6 hours) — moderate complexity
    - **Priority:** High — emergent admission diagnosis

#### **E. ENDOCRINE (MAJOR GAPS)**

20. **Type 1 & Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (Diagnosis, Classification, Chronic Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** ~7% of US population; intern year medication counseling, glucose monitoring, HbA1c interpretation essential
    - **Current coverage:** ~25% (DKA/HHS acute management present; chronic management absent)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Diagnostic thresholds (fasting glucose, 2-hr glucose tolerance, A1C), T1DM vs. T2DM pathophysiology, insulin secretion vs. sensitivity, oral agent classes (metformin, sulfonylureas, DPP-4, GLP-1, SGLT2i), insulin regimens (basal-bolus, premixed), glucose targets by age/comorbidity, hypoglycemia unawareness
    - **Ideal source:** ADA Guidelines, Endocrinology UpToDate
    - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — very high-frequency diagnosis in hospitalized population

21. **Thyroid Disease (Hypothyroidism, Hyperthyroidism, Thyroid Storm)**
    - **Why it matters:** Intern year recognition of thyroid dysfunction; medication management; thyroid storm emergency
    - **Current coverage:** ~10% (minimal; emergency context only)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** TSH/free T4 interpretation, hypothyroidism causes (primary vs. secondary), levothyroxine dosing/absorption/drug interactions, hyperthyroidism causes (Graves, thyroiditis, toxic nodule), antithyroid drugs (PTU, methimazole), beta-blocker use, thyroid storm diagnosis/management, sick euthyroid syndrome
    - **Ideal source:** Endocrinology UpToDate, AACE Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours) — requires comprehensive coverage
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — very common admission diagnosis

22. **Adrenal Insufficiency (Recognition & Steroid Replacement)**
    - **Why it matters:** Rare but high-consequence diagnosis; steroid replacement in critical illness essential
    - **Current coverage:** ~15% (brief mention in context of anesthesia)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Primary vs. secondary adrenal insufficiency distinction, ACTH/cortisol interpretation, acute adrenal crisis recognition, steroid replacement doses (acute vs. chronic), hydrocortisone dosing in sepsis, sick leave steroid adjustment
    - **Ideal source:** Endocrinology UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — low-frequency but high-consequence diagnosis

#### **F. INFECTIOUS DISEASE (MAJOR GAPS)**

23. **Meningitis (Bacterial, Viral, Fungal: Diagnosis & Treatment)**
    - **Why it matters:** High-mortality emergency; empiric antibiotic choice before culture is intern year responsibility
    - **Current coverage:** ~20% (minimal; emergency context)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** CSF analysis interpretation (cell counts, glucose, protein), gram stain/culture/PCR, empiric antibiotics by age + risk factors (ceftriaxone, vancomycin, ampicillin dosing), steroid use, isolation duration, post-exposure prophylaxis

    - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-mortality emergency

24. **Urinary Tract Infection / Urosepsis**
    - **Why it matters:** Common source of sepsis in intern year; empiric antibiotic selection needed
    - **Current coverage:** ~20% (mentioned as sepsis source)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Cystitis vs. pyelonephritis vs. asymptomatic bacteriuria distinction, urinalysis/urine culture interpretation, empiric antibiotic selection (E. coli prevalence, fluoroquinolone vs. TMP-SMX vs. cephalosporin), pregnancy-specific management, catheter-associated UTI, treatment duration
    - **Ideal source:** IDSA UTI Guidelines
    - **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — very high-frequency diagnosis

25. **Antibiotic Selection & Stewardship (Empiric Antibiotics by Presentation)**
    - **Why it matters:** Core intern year responsibility; resistance patterns change; guidelines essential

    - **Sub-topics missing:** Gram-negative coverage (3rd gen cephalosporin, fluoroquinolone, carbapenem indications), gram-positive coverage (vancomycin, linezolid indications), anaerobic coverage (clindamycin, metronidazole), MRSA considerations, local resistance patterns, Clostridium difficile risk with antibiotics, duration of therapy principles
    - **Ideal source:** IDSA Antibiotic Stewardship Guidelines, Sanford Guide
    - **Effort:** High (7–9 hours) — requires systematic framework + local pattern knowledge
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — fundamental intern year prescribing responsibility

26. **Fever Workup & Common Fever Etiologies**

27. **C. difficile Infection (CDI)**

    - **Ideal source:** IDSA C. difficile Guidelines

28. **HIV/AIDS & Opportunistic Infections**

29. **Endocarditis (Diagnosis, Embolic Risk, Management)**

    - **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — serious, low-frequency but high-consequence diagnosis

#### **G. HEMATOLOGY/ONCOLOGY (MAJOR GAPS)**

30. **Anemia (Microcytic, Normocytic, Macrocytic: Differential & Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** Common intern year finding; iron supplementation, B12/folate replacement decisions needed
    - **Current coverage:** ~15% (transfusion present; anemia differential absent)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** MCV interpretation, iron studies (ferritin, TIBC, sat%), reticulocyte count interpretation, B12/folate deficiency (pernicious vs. dietary), hemolysis markers (haptoglobin, LDH, retic count), anemia of chronic disease, thalassemia trait recognition
    - **Ideal source:** Hematology UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — very common finding

31. **Thrombocytopenia (Differential: ITP, TTP, HIT, DIC)**
    - **Why it matters:** Bleeding risk stratification; HIT recognition prevents anticoagulation (dangerous); differential affects management
    - **Current coverage:** ~20% (brief mention in coagulopathy)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** ITP diagnosis (clinical diagnosis, absence of splenomegaly), ITP management (IVIG, corticosteroids, rituximab), HIT pathophysiology + risk (day 4-6 onset), TTP vs. HUS distinction, DIC diagnosis (PT/PTT/fibrinogen/D-dimer), DIC underlying cause management
    - **Ideal source:** Hematology UpToDate
    - **Effort:** High (6–8 hours) — complex decision-making
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — HIT recognition is critical to prevent thrombotic events

32. **Bleeding Disorders (Inherited vs. Acquired, Workup)**
    - **Why it matters:** Preoperative screening; intern year recognition of bleeding phenotype
    - **Current coverage:** ~30% (perioperative coagulopathy present; inherited disorders minimal)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Coagulation cascade, PT/PTT/INR interpretation, von Willebrand disease (diagnosis, desmopressin use), factor deficiencies (VIII, IX, V), natural products (vitamin K, FFP, prothrombin complex), bleeding phenotype (mucosal vs. joint vs. surgical)
    - **Ideal source:** Hematology UpToDate, Coagulation Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (7–9 hours) — requires physiology foundation
    - **Priority:** High — affects surgical planning

#### **H. NEUROLOGY (MAJOR GAPS)**

33. **Stroke (Ischemic & Hemorrhagic: Recognition, Imaging, Thrombolytics/Intervention)**
    - **Why it matters:** Time-critical emergency; thrombolytic window (4.5 hr), thrombectomy window (24 hr); intern year responsibility
    - **Current coverage:** ~15% (brief mention in complications)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Stroke scale scoring (NIHSS), CT vs. MRI interpretation (hemorrhage exclusion), thrombolytic criteria, door-to-needle time, contraindications to thrombolytics, thrombectomy criteria, post-stroke complications (hemorrhagic transformation, cerebral edema, aspiration prevention)
    - **Ideal source:** AHA/ASA Stroke Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (8–10 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — highest-priority emergent diagnosis

34. **Seizures (Generalized vs. Focal: Recognition, Workup, Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** High-frequency emergency; status epilepticus is life-threatening; intern year medication responsibility
    - **Current coverage:** ~10% (minimal mention)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Seizure vs. syncope vs. stroke distinction, EEG interpretation basics, status epilepticus management (benzodiazepines first, phenytoin/levetiracetam second-line), post-seizure workup (labs, imaging, EEG timing), seizure prophylaxis after head injury/stroke, anticonvulsant interactions
    - **Ideal source:** Neurology UpToDate, AES Seizure Guidelines
    - **Effort:** High (7–9 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — high-frequency emergency

35. **Intracranial Pressure / Cerebral Edema (Recognition & Management)**
    - **Why it matters:** Severe complications; osmotic therapy, head positioning, sedation decisions are intern year
    - **Current coverage:** ~30% (neurosurgery context; non-surgical ICU setting weak)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** ICP physiology, signs of increased ICP (headache, pupil changes, altered mental status), mannitol/hypertonic saline dosing, head-of-bed elevation, adequate sedation (avoiding hypertension/hypoxia), target CPP, monitoring modalities (ICP catheter)
    - **Ideal source:** Neurocritical Care UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — serious complication

36. **Altered Mental Status (Differential Diagnosis Beyond Delirium)**
    - **See Section 2-B above; this expands to include encephalitis, CNS infection, metabolic causes, medication-induced**

#### **I. PSYCHIATRY & BEHAVIORAL (MODERATE GAPS)**

37. **Substance Abuse & Withdrawal Syndromes (Alcohol, Opioid, Benzodiazepine)**
    - **Why it matters:** Common intern year admission; alcohol withdrawal is life-threatening; medication dosing essential
    - **Current coverage:** ~25% (alcohol withdrawal + opioid withdrawal mentioned)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Alcohol withdrawal timeline (tremor → seizure → delirium tremens), CIWA-Ar score interpretation, benzodiazepine dosing protocols (lorazepam, diazepam), seizure prophylaxis, thiamine/folate supplementation, addiction medicine consultation, opioid withdrawal management (methadone, buprenorphine), benzodiazepine withdrawal (protracted, serious)
    - **Ideal source:** Addiction Medicine UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (5–7 hours)
    - **Priority:** CRITICAL — common, potentially lethal presentation

38. **Depression & Anxiety Screening/Management**
    - **Why it matters:** Postoperative mood disorders, medication interactions, suicide risk in hospitalized patients
    - **Current coverage:** ~5% (minimal)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** PHQ-9/GAD-7 screening, SSRI/SNRI/TCA indication and side effects, serotonin syndrome recognition, suicidal ideation assessment, psychiatric consult indications, antidepressant drug interactions
    - **Ideal source:** Psychiatry UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Medium (4–6 hours)
    - **Priority:** Medium — affects postop course

39. **Suicide Risk Assessment & Management**
    - **Why it matters:** Intern year consults common; safety planning essential
    - **Current coverage:** <5% (minimal mention in ethical issues)
    - **Sub-topics missing:** Risk stratification (static vs. modifiable factors), Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale, seclusion/restraint indications, psychiatric hospitalization triggers, confidentiality limits (duty to warn)
    - **Ideal source:** Psychiatry UpToDate
    - **Effort:** Low-Medium (3–5 hours)
    - **Priority:** High — critical safety competency

## NOTES FOR CURRICULUM STEWARD

1. **Current strength:** Anesthesiology + ICU critical care coverage is excellent; perioperative + ICU management dominates.
2. **Current weakness:** Foundational medicine, systematic differential diagnosis, and non-perioperative internal medicine are sparse.
3. **Target trajectory:** Build intern year curriculum to 500–700 medicine-focused units (currently 128), proportional to intern year responsibility distribution.
